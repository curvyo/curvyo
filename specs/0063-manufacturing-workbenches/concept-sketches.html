Page kinds and output pages: concept sketches

Wireframes for specs/0063-manufacturing-workbenches/concept-output-pages.md, concept A (operation list with explicit assignment), round 2 after the customer feedback of 2026-11-05. There is no workbench switcher in any sketch: every page shows its kind (glyph and word) next to its name, and the tools and tabs follow the active page. Real app proportions: tool rail card 48 px, Properties panel 32 px strip plus 280 px body, status bar 24 px, output page 400 × 300 mm at 1.6 px/mm unless noted. Colours are the design-system tokens; operation colours --op-1 to --op-6 and the fixture tokens are new (Proposal). The dark values are sketch placeholders, dark mode is not decided. Yellow numbers are annotations, not UI.

Pages stay in the Document tab, the same list on every page kind. Each row shows the page name with its kind (glyph and word); the panel header repeats it for the active page. Add page opens an inline group (no popup) with Kind and, for Laser, the machine profile.

a3 · the same tab with the laser page active: Machine and Fixtures replace Page
  1. 1No workbench switcher. The panel header names the tab, the active page and its kind; the canvas page label and the status bar say the same. The kind decides the tools and tabs, nothing else does.
  2. 2One page list for all kinds. The kind is a glyph plus a word after the name (shape and text, never colour alone). A press on a row activates the page; Ctrl+Page Up / Page Down step through the list (Proposal).
  3. 3Kind is a three-segment switch: Drawing, Sketch, Laser. Only machine kinds whose extension is on appear; later CNC, Plotter, Embroidery join it.
  4. 4Machine list from the profile library (0026). The last picked profile is preselected, so Add is often the only press after Kind.
  5. 5Add (Enter) creates the page at the next free spot in the plane and activates it, one undo step. A laser page gets the work area of the machine; Cancel or Escape closes the group.
  6. 6A laser page has no size fields: the work area comes from the profile and is shown as text. Picking another profile changes it; content keeps its place relative to the origin corner.
  7. 7Fixtures of the machine setup. The check box is Active (the fixture is on the bed now): several may be active at once. Sketch g shows them on the canvas.

d2 · dragging an object from a drawing page onto the laser page
  1. 1While dragging, the page frame is the ghost. Near a slot of fitting size (0.5 mm, also turned 90 degrees) it snaps centre to centre; the slot fills with the accent and a chip names the target.
  2. 2Release: a placed page, live. Its content follows every edit of the Coaster page; its position, rotation and scale belong to the bed.
  3. 3The dragged page row: dimmed with a dashed accent outline. The drag starts after 4 px, Escape cancels, nothing is written until release.
  4. 4The original stays on its page (it snaps back); only a clone is created. Alt at release moves the object itself, Ctrl makes an independent copy (sketch i).
  5. 5The cursor badge says what the drop makes. Snapping and fixture targets apply during the drag as for any move.

B gets right

  • One concept fewer: everything is a layer.
  • Familiar from LightBurn, but no drawing colour is abused.

B gets wrong

  • The Layers tab means two different things on two page kinds.

g2 · the Fixtures block: Active per fixture, grid parameters expanded
  1. 1Engraved grid: thin lines every 10 mm, thicker major lines every 50 mm, so a part is placed by counting fives, not single lines.
  2. 2Measuring tapes along the bottom and left edge, inside the bed: ticks every 5 mm, longer every 10 mm, numbers every 50 mm, counted from the origin corner. On the real board they are engraved with the grid.
  3. 3Clamps over the board edge: keep-out areas, hatched. An operation that crosses one is counted in the summary, never refused.
  4. 4Coaster board, a slide-in board (holder kind) with four engraved coaster outlines. Each outline is a slot: a coaster page dragged near it snaps centre to centre (Coaster 1 and 2 filled, live).
  5. 5Stop: the fence the board is pushed against, so the board and its slots are at a known place every time.
  6. 6A selected fixture: the same box and handles as any object, and the Fixture tag. Moving it moves its slots; the setup then shows “Differs from profile” (ADR 0039 rule).
  7. 7Fixture jobs: a section of their own below Cutting. Output is off by default; turn one on to make the board (or engrave the grid) once, then off again. They are stored with the fixture, so they travel with the machine setup.
  8. 8Selection block of the board: it is in Board outlines only. Fixtures are assigned like any item, but only to fixture jobs; a work operation does not take a fixture (Proposal).
  9. 9Active check box: the fixture is on the bed now. Inactive fixtures (Pen jig) are not drawn, not snapped to and their jobs do not run.
  10. 10The selected fixture's row, the same selection as on the canvas.
  11. 11Grid parameters in place: spacing, major every n lines, tapes on which edges, numbers from the origin.

h · Run order: the Cuts last switch (1.5×)

The list order is the run order, always. The switch Cuts last at the top of the Operations tab is on by default for every new laser page and is stored per laser page. On: two sections, Marking and Cutting, and cutting runs after all marking, so nothing is engraved on a part that has already dropped out. Off: one list, any order.

i · Moving versus editing: objects on the pasteboard, over a sketch page and on the laser page

Laser 1 is active and the Node tool is pressed. Moving (drag, arrow keys, the position, size and angle fields) works on every object everywhere. Editing (nodes, style, text, path operations, drawing into it) works only on objects of the active page’s kind.
