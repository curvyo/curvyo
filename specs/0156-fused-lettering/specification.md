# Bridged lettering: bridges that make lettering and other shapes hold together when cut

Status: Built, awaiting tester and customer acceptance (PR to come). Both slices (Lettering and Stencil) ship in one PR. Fragen 395 to 398 and 403 are answered; 404 is A (lead) (2026-11-26)
Priority: Should
Origin: Customer (2026-11-26, translated): "Fonts and other objects need an operation for lasering that makes them hold together, e.g. so the dots of ü, i, ö, ä do not fall off and letters that stand apart are nicely connected." Answers of 2026-11-26: Frage 392 FUSE-SCOPE **B** (both cut-out and stencil, sliced: cut-out first, stencil second); Frage 393 FUSE-HOW **A** (automatic bridge at the narrowest gap, width in mm, must look nice, not rigidly straight; joined into one cut and still selectable as its own group members); Frage 394 FUSE-LIVE **A** (one-shot action that produces a new object, visible on laser pages only). Answers of 2026-11-26 to the open Fragen: 395 B-like (no chip, no preview, fields in the Select bar), 396 A, 397 A, 398 C, 403 A, 404 A. Everything marked **(Proposal)** is the product owner's and vetoable at the demo.
Revision 2026-11-26: the command is named **Bridge** ("Fuse" in the draft); the architect's spec issues 1 to 11 (`adrs.md`) and the UX engineer's S1 to S10 (`ux-notes.md` section 12) are taken in, see "Decided by the lead (Proposal)". Folder and code names (`fuse/`, `fuse_plan.rs`, undo label `fuse`) belong to the architect; the maker only sees "Bridge".
Depends on: `0068-laserable-lettering` (Weld: one outline, each line once, the kernel), `0016-boolean-operations` (kernel, tolerances, Boolean card), `0074-text-objects` (Object to path), `0098-page-kinds` and `0101-laser-operations` (laser page, assignments, nearest rule), `0023-groups`, `0020-undo-redo`.
Related: `0139-laser-cut-path-options` (tabs, see "How this differs from tabs"), `0029-laser-job-preview-and-output` (output order), `0086-stick-fonts`.

## User value

As a maker who cuts lettering or other thin shapes out of a sheet, or cuts the lettering as a stencil, I want one action that adds smooth bridges where pieces would fall off (the dot of an i, the dots of ü, ö, ä, letters that stand apart) or where the middle of a letter would fall out (the counters of O, A, B, e in a stencil), so that the job comes out of the laser in one piece and looks designed, not patched with rectangles.

## Field reference

What other tools do (from memory, **not re-checked in this session**; the tester or lead may verify before the demo):

| Tool | Cut-out lettering | Stencil islands | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|---|
| LightBurn | The usual route is Offset Shapes outwards, Weld, Offset back, or drawn bars welded by hand. Weld is the join. | Hand-drawn bars subtracted from the letters (Boolean Subtract); stencil fonts with the bridges designed in. | Manual, several steps, rectangles with sharp corners, no connector kept as its own object. |
| Inkscape | Union with a stroke or Linked Offset by hand; Path > Union. | Community extensions for stencil bridges exist; otherwise bars and Difference by hand. | No rule for "narrowest gap", no smooth blend, extension quality varies. |
| Glowforge (Canvas) | Offset or "Unite" of text; the shipped stencil fonts. | Only fonts that already have bridges. | No tool to bridge an arbitrary font. |
| xTool Creative Space | Weld and Offset; text tools with a few fonts. | Stencil fonts, hand bars. | No automatic bridge placement. |
| Stencil fonts (Stardos Stencil and others) | not for cutting out | Bridges are designed into the font. | Only that font; the bridges are fixed in width and place. |

**What we do better.** One action on any selection (text, paths, groups, imported shapes, any font): bridges are placed automatically at the narrowest gap, have a width in mm, blend smoothly into the strokes, and come out as one cut outline (nothing cut twice, as `0068`) plus the connectors as their own selectable members, so the maker can engrave them to darken them or delete one and cut it by hand.

## Words

- **Bridge:** the command and, in the UI, one connector ("Bridge 1"). **Connector** stays the word of this spec for the geometry.
- **Piece:** a connected area of the union of the selected regions (two letters that overlap or touch are one piece; an i is two pieces, the stem and the dot).
- **Gap:** the empty space between two pieces. **Narrowest gap** of two pieces: the pair of boundary points, one on each piece, at the smallest distance; points whose distance lies within the kernel tolerance (0.01 mm) of the minimum tie; the bridge is centred **half way between the middles of the tied stretches of both pieces** (the middle by arc length of each piece's tied stretch), and its ends are the points of each piece nearest that centre; among several tied stretches, the one with the smallest x, then the smallest y (pieces are indexed by the smallest point (x, y) of their outer contour) **(Proposal, architect issue 3, as built; the literal rule put the bars of criterion 9 at y = 6.38 because the tie tolerance lets the stretch run round a corner on one piece only)**. Spanning-forest ties: exactly equal distances only.
- **Connector:** the area a bridge adds in a Cut-out job, or the material a bridge leaves standing in a Stencil job. Its **width** is the width of its narrowest part, the neck.
- **Blend:** the smooth widening of a connector where it meets a piece, tangent to the piece's boundary (a fillet of radius min(Width, 0.45 x the gap), **(Proposal, as built: a radius of the full Width filled every gap narrower than twice the Width into a plug)**).
- **Cut-out** (slice 1; in the UI the mode is called **Lettering**, because "Cut-out" is also an operation kind of `0101`): the lettering is the part; the sheet around it is waste. **Stencil** (slice 2): the lettering is the hole; the sheet is the part.
- **Island** (Stencil): a piece of sheet fully enclosed by lettering, for example the inside of an O: an inner contour of the union of the lettering.
- **Dot piece:** a piece whose area is below a quarter of the area of the **largest piece within Reach** of it, so a lone ü has dots **(Proposal, architect issue 2)**.
- **Laser page:** a page of kind Laser whose extension is on (`0098`, `0028`).
- Tolerances: kernel 0.01 mm, grid 0.001 mm (`0016` criteria 25 and 39). All numbers are mm unless a unit is named.

## How this differs from tabs (`0139`)

`0139` calls them **Tabs**: output gaps, never bridges. Tabs leave a gap in the **output** of a cut path; the drawing is unchanged and the part stays in the sheet. Bridge changes the **drawing**: it makes a new object with bridges as real geometry, so the cut outline is one path, the preview and every export show it, and the connectors can be assigned to another operation. `0068` left bridges out of scope as "drawn geometry in the design"; this is that follow-up. A tab cannot join two pieces of lettering to each other, which is the customer's case; for stencil islands two aligned tabs could leave a bridge but without width control across the stroke or any smooth shape, which is why `0139` does not cover it.

## Acceptance criteria

### Entry point (both slices)

1. Given the active page is a laser page, then the Boolean card of the tool rail has one more button, **Bridge**, after Weld, with the rules of `0016` criteria 1a, 2 and 3 (a command, not a tool; roving focus; no shortcut; the tool does not change). It is enabled when the Select tool is active and one or more objects are selected, and dimmed otherwise like the other buttons. Its tooltip names it and gives the rule line of `ux-notes.md` (no operating hint text). The same **Bridge** button is also in the Select bar, next to the fields of criterion 3.
2. Given the active page is a drawing page, a sketch page, a "Laser (off)" page or a page of an unknown kind, then the card has **no** Bridge button at all (absent, not dimmed; the other buttons do not move), and no menu or command offers Bridge. Switching the active page changes the card in the same frame, as the rail follows the page kind (`0098` criterion 8). Given the card with Bridge in the smallest window, then the rail wraps by itself and nothing is built for it (S3; replaces the 4 px clause, which cannot hold even with Weld).
3. Given the active page is a laser page and Bridge is available for the selection (the rule of criterion 1), then the **Select bar** shows a Bridge group with the fields of the slice (criteria 5, 21), a **Join all** switch (Frage 398 C) and the button **Bridge**. There is no panel, no chip and no canvas preview (Frage 395, customer answer 2026-11-26): pressing Bridge on the Boolean card or in the Select bar applies at once with the current field values. The values are an app preference (not in the document), kept as typed on commit. Bridge is one-shot: the result is a new object with no link back to the fields (Frage 394 A). Refusals show as the usual rail notice (criterion 17).
4. Given a selection that spans several pages, or leaves that are not on the active laser page, or a selection that holds a placement (a placed page is not a drawing object, architect issue 8), then Bridge is refused (a leaf off the laser page gives this sentence, not the generic move-only refusal) with "Bridge works on one laser page. Nothing was changed." and nothing changes **(Proposal)**.

### Slice 1: Cut-out (customer idea, Frage 392 B first)

5. Given the Select bar in slice 1, then the Bridge group has two fields: **Width** (0.2 to 10 mm, default 1.0, **(Proposal)**) and **Reach** (0.5 to 50 mm, default 5.0, **(Proposal)**; Frage 398). Typed values outside the range are clamped on commit, with the limit notice of `0141` ("Width set to 10 mm, the maximum.") shown for 3 s under the Bridge group of the Select bar in the status look (S9). The Bridge group also has the switch **Join all** (default off): when on, every piece is joined regardless of Reach (Reach is read as unbounded). Values are numbers in mm only.
6. Given a selection of texts, groups (at any depth), paths, compound paths, rectangles, ellipses, polygons and stars, then all their leaves are operands and are read as Weld reads them (`0068` Terms: nonzero region, stacking order of the document, text converted as by Object to path in the same history step, open operands closed by a straight line); the maker is not asked. The Outline is the **Union** (`0016`) of the pieces and the connectors, not Weld's result: Weld keeps inner lines where letters overlap, and cutting them would part the letters (architect issue 1, `0068` correction 4). The result is **no longer an editable text**; Ctrl+Z restores the text.
7. Given the operands, then Bridge finds the pieces (union of the regions) and connects them as follows, deterministically: (a) every dot piece is connected only to its nearest piece that is not a dot piece, within Reach, and never links two letters (a dot piece is never the middle of a connection between two non-dot pieces; **(Proposal, lead decision)**) (a dot with no non-dot piece within Reach, a lone colon, takes part in (b) like any piece, issue 7); (b) the remaining pieces are connected by the shortest connections between them (a minimum spanning forest of the narrowest gaps, ties by the order of criterion `Words`, Gap), each connection only if its narrowest gap is at most Reach (with Join all on, whatever the gap). A piece is therefore connected to a neighbour at **one** connector; no cycles are made. A piece beyond Reach of every other piece stays apart and is still part of the result, unmarked. A piece inside a hole of another piece is not bridged in Lettering mode; it stays apart and in the result, silently (issue 6).
8. Given an i built of a stem 2 x 10 mm at (0, 0) to (2, 10) and a disc of radius 1 mm centred at (1, 13), with Width 1.0 and Reach 5, then one connector is made whose centre line runs from (1, 10) to (1, 12) within 0.01 mm, whose neck is 1.0 mm wide within 0.01 mm, and the Outline (criterion 11) has one closed outline whose area is at least 1.5 mm² and at most 4.0 mm² larger than stem plus disc (20 mm² + pi mm²).
9. Given two bars 2 x 10 mm, the first at (0, 0) to (2, 10) and the second at (5, 3) to (7, 13), then the narrowest gap is 3.0 mm and its bridge is centred at y = 6.5 within 0.01 mm (half way between the middles of the tied stretches, see Words, Gap).
10. Given a ü set in a font fixed for the test (a font file in `tests/fixtures/`), then exactly two connectors are made, each from one dot to the u, and none between the two dots (rule 7a). Given the same for i, ö and ä, the same holds with the counts 1, 2 and 2.
11. Given a result, then the selection is replaced by **one group** named "Bridged lettering", at the place of the top-most operand in the stacking order, holding exactly two members, in this order, Outline on top and Bridges below it in the Layers tree: (a) **Outline**, one object (a path, or a compound path when several outlines exist) that is the union of all pieces and all connectors (outer outlines and inner contours of the pieces, with holes wound as holes, `0016` criterion 41), and (b) **Bridges**, a group of one closed path per connector, named "Bridge 1", "Bridge 2", ... in the order of criterion 18. One undo step named "Bridge". Style: the Outline takes the style of Weld's result (`0068`) and the Bridges the same (S10; the architect confirms with criterion 20). In the Layers tree the result is expanded one level and Bridges is collapsed. The result is selected.
12. Given the Outline, then every visible line exists once: no two of its segments overlap over more than 0.001 mm, no part of a connector's boundary lies inside a piece (the connector joins the outline, it is not drawn over it), and the Outline's area equals the union of the pieces and the connectors within 0.01 mm times the total outline length (property test on at least 200 random shape sets, fixed seed, as `0068` criterion 14; it holds by construction as the Outline is a Union). The tester also checks, from the exported job (`0029`) of the Outline in a Cut operation, that the cut length equals the Outline's perimeter within 0.01 mm.
13. Given the Bridges, then each connector region is exactly the added material: it does not overlap any piece by more than the tolerance (area of the overlap at most 0.01 mm times the connector's boundary length), the union of the pieces and the Bridges equals the Outline within the same bound, and each connector is its own selectable object, so the maker can select the group Bridges or one bridge and assign it to an Engrave operation in `0101` (the nearest rule of `0101` criterion 12 applies: an assignment on a member beats the group's).
14. Given the source operands carried operations (`0101`), then the Outline carries the **effective** assignment (nearest rule of `0101` criterion 12) of the bottom-most operand, so an assignment inherited from its group counts (architect issue 9), written on the Outline itself. The Bridges carry none: they are Not assigned (the "Not assigned" check of `0029` criterion 5 counts them like any item) and the maker assigns them explicitly (Frage 396, default A). The Bridges carry no assignment of their own; under a group or layer that holds an assignment they inherit it by the nearest rule of `0101` criterion 12, like any leaf, and the Operations view shows it (Frage 404 A).
15. Given the result, then no connector crosses a piece other than the two it joins: a connector whose band would add area inside a third piece is not made, and the two pieces are connected through the third piece (rule 7 with obstruction). Test: three pieces in a row with the middle one narrower than the others give two connectors, never one connector through the middle piece.
16. Given a connector, then it looks designed: (a) its axis is straight when the boundary normals at the two narrowest-gap points lie along the line joining them within 5 degrees (the ordinary dot-to-stem case) and otherwise a curve that leaves each piece along that piece's boundary normal and has no inflection except one; (b) its neck is the Width everywhere along the axis within 0.01 mm except in the Blend, where it widens tangent to each piece; (c) at each junction with a piece the outline turns by at most 15 degrees between consecutive nodes, measured on the nodes the connector adds plus its two seam nodes, not on the operand's own flattened curves (a 1 mm dot flattened at 0.01 mm turns about 16 degrees per node; issue 5); the Blend radius is min(Width, 0.45 x the gap) and the Blend arcs are built at no more than 10 degrees per step (no sharp corner where the bridge meets a stroke); (d) with Blend the same as Width, a connector between a vertical and a horizontal stroke ends tangent to both. A golden file in `tests/fixtures/` holds the outline of the connectors for these cases.
17. Given no two pieces lie within Reach of each other, or the selection is one piece, then Bridge is refused with "Bridge: nothing to join. Nothing was changed." Given an operand that is not finite or beyond `MAX_COORDINATE_MM`, or an operand that encloses no area, then the refusals of `0016` criteria 16 and 17a apply. Given a result with more than 2000 connectors, then "Bridge would make more than 2000 bridges. Nothing was changed." Refusals show as the rail notice. There is no success notice (customer rule of 2026-10-31, `docs/design-system.md`).
18. Given the result, then it is deterministic: the same document and settings give a byte-identical group (same node order, same names), tested by running twice and by a golden file for the i, the ü and the bars of criterion 9. Bridge paths are named "Bridge n" in the order of their first point.
19. Given a selection of 60 pieces and 3000 nodes then Bridge takes at most 300 ms of thread CPU time, end to end, measured natively in release as every other budget is (`CLAUDE.md` §5, never wall time; architect issue 10) **(Proposal)**.
20. Given save and reopen, then the group, Outline and Bridges are ordinary objects, saved and reopened with no new object kind and no change of `format_version` **(Proposal; architect decides)**; a file saved before this slice opens unchanged. Given the Operations view (`0101` criterion 18), then the Outline is drawn by its operation and the Bridges by theirs or dimmed as Not assigned, like any items.

### Slice 2: Stencil (customer idea, Frage 392 B second)

21. Given the Select bar in slice 2, then the Bridge group has a field **Mode** with the values **Lettering** (default, slice 1) and **Stencil** (S7), and in Stencil mode the fields **Width** (as criterion 5) and **Bridges per island** (1 to 4, default 2; Frage 397 A). Reach and Join all do not apply to Stencil and are hidden (not disabled).
22. Given Stencil mode, then the selection is read as in criterion 6 and is the **hole** shape: the union of the regions is the area that will be cut away. Every inner contour of that union (a counter or any enclosed piece of sheet) is an island. Given none, then Bridge is refused with "Bridge: no islands. Nothing was changed."
23. Given an island, then Bridge makes the chosen number of bridges, each across the lettering between the island and the surrounding sheet at its narrowest crossing, measured along the island's edge normal into the stroke (sampled every 0.05 mm and at every vertex): the first at the narrowest crossing (ties: the top-most, then the left-most); each further one is the **narrowest** among the crossings whose middle is at least a quarter of the island's perimeter away along the island's boundary from every earlier bridge of that island, and among ties the one **farthest along the boundary** from the earlier bridges, then the top-most, then the left-most (architect issue 4; this makes criterion 24 hold). A bridge never crosses another lettering contour except the stroke it bridges; where the nearest sheet beyond the stroke is another island, that island is connected to the sheet through it in turn (island chains, as in the two counters of a B or the inside of a nested ring).
24. Given an O made of a disc of radius 10 mm minus a concentric disc of radius 6 mm (stroke 4 mm, lettering = ring), Width 1.0 and Bridges per island 2, then two bridges are made, centred at (0, -8) and (0, 8) (page coordinates, y down; the first at the top by the ties, the second at the bottom as the farthest along the boundary, rule of criterion 23) within 0.01 mm, each 1.0 mm wide within 0.01 mm, and the Outline is two closed outlines (the two C shapes) with **no inner contour**; its area is the ring's area pi times (100 minus 36) minus the area of the two connectors, within the bound of criterion 12.
25. Given a B, an A, an e and an 8 in a font fixed for the test, then the Outline has no inner contour after Bridge, the number of connectors is Bridges per island times the number of islands (B: 2 islands, A: 1, e: 1, 8: 2), and every island is connected to the sheet by at least one connector.
26. Given a result in Stencil mode, then the group is named "Bridged stencil", holds **Outline** (the lettering holes minus the bridges, the line the laser cuts, each line once) and **Bridges** (one closed path per bridge, the material that stays standing, so it is selectable and can be engraved to show it), and the rules of criteria 11 to 14 and 18 to 20 apply with "added material" read as "standing material".
27. Given a bridge, then it has the look rules of criterion 16 with the neck across the stroke and the Blend widening towards the island and the sheet; its axis is a straight line along the island's edge normal (as built). A bridge's width runs along the stroke, so a thin stroke does not limit it; a bridge is never wider than a quarter of its island's perimeter (architect issue 11; the clamp of the draft is dropped) **(Proposal)**.
28. Given the result of Stencil mode cut from a sheet, then the cutting order of `0029` criterion 22 holds unchanged: the Outline has no inner contours, so no island is cut free.

## Out of scope

- A live, re-editable fused object (Frage 394 A: one-shot only); a link back to the originals.
- Choosing a single bridge position by hand on the canvas (handles on a connector) and editing a connector's width after the fact; the maker moves the nodes with the Node tool as for any path, or undoes and runs Bridge again.
- Fonts and shapes changing after the fact: a text is converted (criterion 6); making a stencil font from a font file for the Fonts tab (`0062`, `0086`).
- Automatic fitting of the width to the stroke in Cut-out mode, kerf compensation, strength estimates, grain direction, per-material minimum bridge width.
- Bridging pieces **inside** a hole of another piece in Cut-out mode, and islands that contain further islands beyond the chain of criterion 23.
- Output gaps (Tabs, `0139`), lead-in and overcut.
- Machines other than the laser; other page kinds (Frage 394 A).
- Any operating hint text in the UI (customer rule): the Select bar group and the Boolean card carry only labels naming a control or state.

## Decisions (Fragen, all answered 2026-11-26)

- Frage 395 (FUSE-UI): **B-like.** No panel, chip or preview; Bridge applies at once. Width, Reach, Join all, Mode and Bridges are fields in the Select bar; the button is on the Boolean card and in the Select bar (criteria 1, 3).
- Frage 396 (FUSE-OPS): **A.** Outline takes the effective operation of the bottom-most source object, Bridges none (criterion 14).
- Frage 397 (FUSE-STENCIL-COUNT): **A.** Two bridges per island by default, 1 to 4 (criterion 21).
- Frage 398 (FUSE-REACH): **C.** Reach (default 5 mm) plus a Join all switch in the Select bar (criteria 5, 7).
- Frage 403 (FUSE-RAIL): customer 2026-11-26: the rail wraps by itself, nothing built for it (criterion 2).
- Frage 404 (BRIDGE-INHERIT): **A.** Bridges carry no assignment and inherit by the nearest rule of `0101` criterion 12; no refusal (criterion 14).
