# Gradient fill: a third fill paint that shades closed shapes, open paths and text from colour to colour, linear or radial, with any number of stops and handles on the shape

Status: Draft
Priority: Should
Origin: Customer (decision on Frage 227 C of `0123`, 2026-11-17: "every closed shape gets a third fill paint, Gradient, next to Colour and Pattern; pull it forward as its own slice before `0123`, because it would blow up `0123`"; answers of 2026-11-18 to Frage 232 to 236, see "Decided questions"). Everything marked **(Proposal)** is the product owner's and vetoable at the demo. No question is open.

## User value

As a maker I want to fill a shape with a shade through any number of colours, linear or radial, set by handles on the shape, so that I can draw soft areas, bring Inkscape gradients in and out, and so that a shaded shape can later be engraved as dots on a laser page (`0123`), without a second program for shading.

**What other tools do.**
- **Inkscape**: Fill and Stroke has a Linear/Radial gradient button; the gradient lives in a shared `<defs>` vector, edited with a Gradient tool (G) whose handles sit on the canvas; stops are added by double-click; every gradient is also an SVG element, so it round-trips.
- **Illustrator and Affinity**: a gradient panel and an annotator bar on the shape with stops, angle and midpoints.
- **LightBurn**: no gradient object; shaded areas come from imported bitmaps.
- **What we do better.** The gradient is a fill paint like Colour and Pattern (`0071`), switched in the same place, kept when switched away from, and edited by handles on the shape in the Select tool (no second tool), in the shape's own box so it moves, scales, rotates and mirrors with it. It is plain data (no pixels), imports and exports as real SVG `linearGradient` and `radialGradient` with all stops that Inkscape opens, and on a laser page it is previewed as grey and is what `0123` reads as lightness.

## Words (shown in the UI)

- **Gradient:** the third value of the Fill paint switch, after None, Solid (shown as Colour) and Pattern. A shade between colour stops, Linear or Radial (Frage 233, derived from Frage 236).
- **Stop:** a colour (8-digit RGBA hex, as every colour) at a position in percent along the gradient line. A gradient has two to N stops with **no upper limit** (Frage 232 B); the first is at 0 % and the last at 100 %, in order, equal positions allowed (a hard edge).
- **Gradient line:** Linear: the line between the two end handles; the shade runs along it, perpendicular lines have one colour. Radial: from the centre handle to the radius handle; the shade runs outward, circles have one colour. Before the first and after the last stop the end colours continue.
- **Handle:** a point on the canvas that moves the gradient or a stop; shown in the Select tool for a selected shape with a Gradient fill: Linear two end handles, Radial a centre and a radius handle, plus one dot per inner stop on the line.
- **Angle:** (Linear) the direction of the gradient line in degrees, 0 pointing right, positive counter-clockwise on the page (as `0111` Scan angle). **Radius:** (Radial) the distance centre to radius handle, in mm.

One-line meaning (customer rule: the meaning of a new fill before it is built): **Gradient:** "Shades the inside of the shape through colours along a line, or outward from a centre, that you can drag."

## Acceptance criteria

"Fill paint", "PaintRow", "Pattern", "seed", "style register" are as in `0071`; "linked clone", "own style" as in `0049` and `0057`; "working image" as in `0058`; "laser page", "Engrave", "leaf" as in `0101` and `0029`. All lengths mm, angles degrees.

### The paint

1. Given a closed shape (rectangle, ellipse, polygon or star, path, compound path, linked clone), an **open path** or a **text object** selected (Frage 234 B), when the maker opens Properties > Style > Fill, then the Fill paint switch has the choices None, Colour, Pattern, Gradient (`0071` criterion 1 gains one item, symbol-only with a tooltip: "No fill", "Solid fill", "Pattern fill", "Gradient fill"). Images and groups do not offer it. There is no rail tool and no top bar control for gradients.
2. Given Gradient is chosen on a shape that has none, then the fill starts Linear with two stops, Start = the shape's current Colour (alpha kept) and End = white (`#FFFFFFFF`), at Angle 0 across the shape's box, so the shade is visible at once (Proposal), as one undo step "Fill: Gradient". Switching to None, Colour or Pattern keeps the gradient and all its stops; choosing Gradient again brings it back as it was, with no undo needed (the rule of `0071` UX notes).
3. Given a shape with a Gradient fill, then the Style tab shows, where the Colour block is today, a Gradient block: a two-button Kind switch Linear / Radial (symbol-only, tooltips "Linear", "Radial"; switching keeps the stops and sets default geometry for the new kind); the **stop list**, one row per stop in order, each with a colour swatch, an eyedropper and an 8-digit hex field exactly as the Colour row (`0017`; no new picker), a Position field in percent (fixed 0 and 100 on the first and last row, so shown as text there), and a Remove button (symbol `Trash2`, tooltip "Remove stop", hidden when only two stops are left); an Add stop button (symbol `Plus`, tooltip "Add stop": a stop in the middle of the widest gap, in the shade's colour at that place); a Reverse button (symbol `ArrowLeftRight`, tooltip "Reverse": the order and the positions mirror); and Linear a number field Angle (-180.0 to 180.0, one decimal, unit inside, "deg"), Radial a number field Radius (mm, unit inside, above 0). A Position is clamped between its neighbours; stops never reorder by typing. The inline colour picker of `0017` opens under the row being edited. No sliders, no captions beyond the field names (customer lessons).
4. Given the geometry, then it is stored relative to the shape's box (x and y as fractions of the box width and height, 0 to 1 inside the box, free beyond it, two decimals shown as percent in the handle tooltip only): Linear two end points, defaulting to the middle of the left edge (Start) and the middle of the right edge (End); Radial a centre (default the box centre) and a radius point (default the middle of the right edge), the circle drawn in the box frame; an elliptical radial read from SVG (criterion 14) keeps its second radius as a ratio, drawn exactly, with no panel field and kept by a handle drag (Proposal). Changing Angle turns the line about its midpoint and keeps its length; Radius moves the radius point along its direction; a typed or dragged handle sets Angle and Radius from the geometry (criterion 6).
5. Given the shade, then colour is interpolated linearly in sRGB, channel by channel, alpha included (premultiplied: Proposal, so a fade to transparent has no grey fringe), between the two neighbouring stops; positions before the first and after the last stop take the end colour (`spreadMethod` pad). Tests: Start `#000000FF`, End `#FFFFFFFF` over a box 100 wide at Angle 0 reads 50 % grey at x = 50 within 1/255 per channel; three stops black, red, white at 0, 50, 100 % read pure red at x = 50; a Radial from a centre reads the same colour on every point of one circle within 1/255.

### Editing on the canvas

6. Given a shape with a Gradient fill selected with the Select tool, when the maker looks at the canvas, then the handles are drawn on the gradient line, joined by a thin line, each a round dot filled with its stop colour and ringed in the selection colour (no new colour; the existing handle look of `0009`), the Start (Linear) or centre (Radial) handle marked by a small square so the direction is readable without colour; inner stops are smaller dots on the line. Dragging an end handle moves that end, the angle and length follow; dragging an inner stop dot moves its position along the line, clamped between its neighbours; **double-click on the gradient line adds a stop** there, in the shade's colour at that place; Delete on a selected inner stop removes it (down to two stops). Shift locks the angle to the product's angle stops (15 and 22.5 degrees, horizontal and vertical included, as `0072`); Alt drags the whole line (moves it); Escape while dragging restores the previous state. One drag is one undo step "Edit gradient"; adding or removing a stop is "Add gradient stop" and "Remove gradient stop". The handles never hide the shape's own handles: when both lie within 8 px the gradient handle loses the click (Proposal), and the gradient handles appear only while the Fill switch is Gradient.
7. Given a handle, when the maker clicks it, then the Style tab focuses that stop's row (keyboard route: Tab order Fill switch, Kind, stop rows, Add stop, Reverse, Angle or Radius); a colour typed there previews on the canvas and commits as one step "Edit gradient". Given a **double-click on an end handle** (Linear) or the radius handle (Radial), then an inline entry box opens at the handle, as the existing chips and entry boxes do, with the Angle (Linear) or Radius (Radial) field and the stop's colour swatch; Enter commits, Escape cancels. Given a **double-click on a stop** (an inner stop dot or the Radial centre), then an inline entry box opens with the colour swatch, which opens the colour picker popover of `0017`, and the Position field for an inner stop (Frage 235 A plus).
8. Given a selection of several shapes with Gradients, then the panel shows the fields common to all and "Mixed" where they differ (as `0071` UX note), the stop list only when all have the same number of stops and kind (else one line "Mixed gradients"), and the handles are drawn for the first selected shape only (Proposal); typing a colour, Angle or Radius sets it on all in one step. A Reverse is applied to each shape's own gradient.

### Where it shows

9. Given the editor, then the gradient is drawn in the WebGL path of `curvyo-render-core` as a shaded fill clipped to the shape's outline with holes (even-odd or nonzero as the shape's fill rule; an open path closed by a straight line from its last to its first node, as its Colour fill is; the glyph outlines of a text object), at every zoom, with no banding visible at 8 bits and with the stroke drawn over it; hit-testing of the interior (click to select) works wherever the gradient has alpha above 0 at the point, as for a Colour fill with alpha (Proposal).
10. Given a drawing page, then the gradient is shown in its colours. Given a laser page (`0098`), then the same gradient is shown as grey: each pixel's colour is converted to its luminance (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B on the gamma-encoded values, over white; the weights `0123` criterion 6 uses), because on a laser page colour does not output (`0101` criterion 24) and the grey is what the laser engraves in `0123`. The Fill block still shows the real colours; only the canvas and the thumbnails of a laser page are grey (Proposal: a notice-free change of view, no new setting).
11. Given PNG export, then the gradient is rendered into the PNG at the export resolution, with the same colours as the canvas on a drawing page (grey is a view only: PNG always uses colour).
12. Given a laser job (`0029`), then a Gradient fill changes nothing in it: the fill paint never counts (`0101` criterion 24); the shape keeps its vector hatch exactly as a Colour-filled shape. Engraving a shape as dots by its gradient is `0123` and not this story.
13. Given SVG export (`0024`), then a shape with a Gradient fill is written with `fill="url(#gradient-n)"` and one `linearGradient` or `radialGradient` in `defs` with `gradientUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`, `x1 y1 x2 y2` (Linear) or `cx cy r fx fy` (Radial) in the exported coordinates (the box fractions resolved against the shape's box, then the shape's transform applied as `gradientTransform` only where a rotation/skew exists, and for an elliptical radial), one `stop` element per stop (`offset`, `stop-color`, `stop-opacity`), in the structure Inkscape writes and opens (stops directly in the gradient, ids `gradient-n`); ids are stable and numbered in document order so two exports are byte-identical. A shape that also has a stroke keeps it; a text object keeps its element and gets the `fill`. Test: golden files `curvyo-svg-core/tests/fixtures/export/gradient_linear.svg`, `gradient_multistop.svg` (7 stops, one hard edge), `gradient_radial.svg`, `gradient_ellipse_rotated.svg`, `gradient_compound.svg`, `gradient_open_path.svg`, `gradient_text.svg`; each read back by the reader in the test and by a second, independent parser of the tester's making; at the demo the tester also opens them in Inkscape when it is installed.
14. Given SVG import (`0024`, `docs/svg-import.md`; Frage 236), then a `fill` that refers to a `linearGradient` or `radialGradient` becomes a Gradient fill with **every stop** (any number, `offset`, `stop-color`, `stop-opacity` into alpha, `style="stop-color:..."` as well as attributes), with: `href` and `xlink:href` chains up to 16 links (stops and attributes inherited from the nearest link that has them, as Inkscape writes its vector gradient plus a per-object link); `gradientUnits` `objectBoundingBox` and `userSpaceOnUse`; `gradientTransform` of translation, scale, rotation and skew (a skewed or unequally scaled radial becomes the ellipse of criterion 4); `fx fy` focal points are read as the centre (Proposal; the notice counts them); stops are normalised so the first is at 0 % and the last at 100 % by adding a stop of the end colour; offsets clamped to 0..1 and made monotonic as SVG says. `spreadMethod` `pad` is read as is; `reflect` and `repeat` are drawn as `pad` and the import notice counts them ("1 gradient repeat was shown as a plain shade."). The rule of `curvyo-svg-core/src/paint.rs` `gradient_middle` is removed, and no gradient is simplified to a colour any more. Goldens: `curvyo-svg-core/tests/fixtures/svg_real/inkscape_linear_href.svg`, `inkscape_radial_transform.svg`, `inkscape_multistop_chain.svg`, `inkscape_userspace_units.svg`, `inkscape_stop_opacity.svg` (written as Inkscape writes them) and `illustrator_gradient_*.svg`, one of each case above, checked by tests.

### Live model and operations

15. Given a Gradient shape, when the maker moves, scales, rotates, mirrors, skews or edits its nodes, then the gradient follows: it belongs to the shape's box in its own coordinate frame, so the picture inside the shape is unchanged by a transform and a node edit that changes the box re-fits the gradient to the new box (the points are fractions, so they stay on the same relative place) in the same frame, with no extra step. A resize drag previews the gradient live. A text object's box is its text box, so a change of text re-fits it.
16. Given copy and paste, duplicate, and Paste style (the style copy of `0017`/`0071`), then the copy has the same Gradient (kind, all stops, fractions, Angle); Paste style onto another shape sets that shape's fill to the Gradient with the same fractions in the target's box. Editing the copy does not change the original.
17. Given a linked clone (`0049`) of a Gradient shape, then it shows the gradient of its original, in the clone's frame, and follows edits of the original; with own style (`0057`) it carries its own gradient or colour like any style; the Spread colours action of `0057` leaves gradients alone and says so in its existing skip line (Proposal).
18. Given undo and redo, then every edit of a Gradient (paint switch, colour, Angle, handle drag, Reverse) is one step with its name ("Fill: Gradient", "Edit gradient", "Reverse gradient") and the previous state returns exactly; no gradient edit is applied twice or lost with a History branch (`0042`).
19. Given Object to path, Boolean operations, Combine, Split, Break apart, Offset and Fracture, then a result that is a closed shape keeps the Gradient of the first (bottom) input where `0016` keeps its fill colour, and the gradient fractions are re-based to the result's box (Proposal; same rule as the Colour); Flatten and Weld keep each piece's own. Given a group, then it has no fill of its own and nothing changes (`0023`).
20. Given an open path, then the Gradient item is offered and the gradient is **drawn**: the fill closes the path with a straight line from its last to its first node, the same as the Colour fill does, over the path's box (Frage 234 B). Pattern on an open path stays as `0071` criterion 23 (kept, nothing drawn, no error). No stroke ever has a gradient (out of scope).
21. Given a text object (`0074`), then it has a Gradient fill (Frage 234 B): one shade across the text's box, clipped to the glyph outlines, at every zoom; a mixed selection of text and shapes edits as criterion 8; after Object to path the pieces keep the gradient with fractions re-based (criterion 19). The text's own other attributes are untouched. Images and groups still have none (the Gradient item is not in the tree for them; a mixed selection shows the existing skip line "1 image cannot have a gradient and was not changed.", wording of `0071`).

### Model, format, performance

22. Given the model, then a Gradient fill is style data of the shape: the style registers `fill_paint` value `gradient` and `fill_gradient` (kind Linear or Radial, the stops as a list of colour as 8-digit hex plus position, the geometry as fractions, the radial ratio; nothing else); no pixel, texture or generated vector is stored; the Colour and the Pattern registers stay untouched when Gradient is chosen. A damaged value (unknown colour, fraction not a number, fewer than two stops, unordered positions) is refused on read like `0071`'s keys: the shape is shown with no fill, the file opens, and the damage is counted in the existing notice. The architect decides the register names, the codec and whether a `format_version` bump is needed (Proposal: yes, one bump, the number taken at merge, "next free at merge" in the README table; an older build would draw no fill where the gradient is). Golden project files: `gradient_fill_vNN.curvyo` (one of every kind including an open path and a text object with a Gradient, Linear and Radial, a 9-stop gradient, a linked clone, an own-style clone) and `gradient_fill_damaged_vNN.curvyo` (an unknown colour, a fraction `abc`, a single stop). Curvyo is not deployed, so there is no migration.
23. Given the data of `0007` and `0017` criterion 53 (gradient data in old files, ignored since `0017`), then it stays ignored: this slice does not read it, and an old file opens as it does today (Proposal; old files are not a concern).
24. Given the renderer, performance, and thread CPU time (`curvyo-editor-wasm/tests/cpu_time/`; the architect confirms the limits; Proposal): (a) dragging a handle on a shape with a Gradient redraws within 16 ms per pointer move at 1,000 gradient shapes of 4 stops on the page; (b) a page with 1,000 gradient shapes of 4 stops draws within the frame budget of today's page of 1,000 Colour shapes plus 2 ms; (c) the Style tab's Gradient block appears within 16 ms of choosing Gradient, with 16 stops as well; (d) opening a project with 10,000 gradient shapes takes no longer than 10,000 Colour shapes plus 5 %; (e) there is no upper limit on stops, so a gradient of 256 stops draws and edits without error and within 2 ms more per frame than one of 4 stops (the architect decides the ramp storage).
25. Given crates, then the gradient types, the colour interpolation and the SVG mapping live in `*-core` crates (`curvyo-document-core`, `curvyo-render-core`, `curvyo-svg-core`) with no filesystem, network, clock, thread or UI and `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`; this slice adds no crate and no dependency (Proposal; the architect confirms), no `unsafe`, and no trait without a second implementation. Colours are the existing colour newtype; fractions and angles are newtypes, no bare `f64`.
26. Given the reversal of an accepted decision, then the repository states it: `docs/requirements.md` R-EDIT-006 reads "none, solid colour, pattern (`0071`) or gradient (`0124`)", `docs/design-system.md` gets a "Gradient (`0124`)" row (the UX engineer writes it), `docs/svg-import.md` replaces its gradient sentence, and a case-insensitive search for `gradient` in the Rust crates is no longer expected to find only the legacy path (`0017` criterion 52 is replaced by this story's tests).

## Out of scope

- Conic, diamond and mesh gradients (mesh and conic SVG paint servers are not read; the shape gets no fill and the import notice counts them).
- `spreadMethod` `reflect` and `repeat` (drawn as `pad`, counted in the import notice; a later story if the customer asks), focal points of radial gradients (read as the centre).
- A gradient on a stroke, on an image, on a group, on the document background, or on the canvas background.
- A gradient tool in the tool rail, a gradient panel of its own, saved gradient presets or a library of gradients, colour palettes (`color-management`), midpoint (bias) handles between stops.
- Dithering, engraving a gradient, the laser power ramp, the Engrave test grid: all in `0123`.
- A gradient in the plotter, CNC or embroidery outputs.
- Reading the legacy gradient data of `0007` (23).
- Other interpolation than linear sRGB (linear light, Oklab, easing).
- Animating a gradient, a gradient follow-the-path or any other generator.

## Decided questions

Answered by the customer on 2026-11-18; recorded as decided.

- **Frage 232 (GRAD-STOPS): B, with no limit on the number of stops (customer).** Two to N stops; double-click on the gradient line adds one; a stop list in the panel; stops can be removed down to two (criteria 3, 6, 24e).
- **Frage 233 (GRAD-KIND): not answered explicitly; B, linear and radial (Proposal derived from Frage 236).** The SVG answer asks for `radialGradient` import and export, which needs a radial fill; so a Linear / Radial switch, a centre handle and a radius handle (criteria 3, 4, 6, 13, 14). Vetoable at the demo.
- **Frage 234 (GRAD-SCOPE): B plus open paths (customer).** Text objects get a gradient (text fill, one shade across the text's box); open paths take a gradient and are treated as closed, the fill closing them with a straight line from the last to the first node like their Colour fill, and it is drawn, unlike Pattern. Images and groups stay out (criteria 1, 20, 21).
- **Frage 235 (GRAD-HANDLES): A plus (customer).** Handles on the shape, colour rows and Angle in the Style tab, and a double-click on a handle opens an inline entry box (like the existing chips and entry boxes): the Angle on the line's end handle, a colour swatch (the picker popover) on a stop (criterion 7). The wiring of the entry boxes to the radius is a Proposal.
- **Frage 236 (GRAD-SVG): customer.** Import reads `linearGradient` and `radialGradient` with any number of stops, `gradientTransform`, `gradientUnits` (both), `href` inheritance, `stop-opacity`, and `spreadMethod` `pad` (`reflect` and `repeat` shown as `pad`, counted in the notice: a safe default, no question); Inkscape-compatible, with `inkscape_*.svg` goldens; export writes real `linearGradient` and `radialGradient` that Inkscape opens (criteria 13, 14).
