# Mirror and negative scale: two flip buttons, and scaling over to the other side

Status: Ready (2026-10-14, customer decisions applied: PERCENT, ZERO-TYPED superseded by ZERO-RECOVER; no open questions)
Priority: Should
Origin: Customer (request of 2026-10-14, decisions of 2026-10-14). Criterion 4 is marked (Proposal): it is the product owner's addition, built with the slice, and the customer can veto it at the demo, as with `0044` and `0047`. It is not needed for the request.
Depends on: `0019-multi-object-transform` and `0047-transform-polish` merged (both are: the group box, the shared scale map, the size chip, the true bounding box) and `0020-undo-redo` merged (one gesture = one step). Not blocked by `0049-linked-clone` or `0050-layers-and-objects-panel`; the criteria that touch clones and layers say what they need (criteria 24 and 25). Touches `transform_math.rs`, `group_transform.rs`, `transform_entry.rs`, `group_entry.rs` and the Select bar, so it does not run beside another slice that changes the resize handles, the size chip or the Select bar (`adrs.md`: none of the Ready slices does today).

## User value

As a maker I want to mirror the selected objects left to right or top to bottom with one click, so that the left and right halves of a part, a mirrored bracket or a part cut on the back of the material take one action and not a hand-built negative scale.

As a maker I want to drag a resize handle past the opposite edge and have the objects turn over, and to type a negative width or height, so that scaling never stops at a wall at zero and "make it 30 mm wide on the other side" is one gesture.

The customer's words (translated, 2026-10-14): "Add a feature to mirror the selected object(s): in the Select tool simply two symbol buttons for flipping horizontally and vertically. At the same time extend scaling so that you can scale over to the other side, in all modes. Inkscape does this too; today with us it stops at 0. For double-click and typed value, negative values must therefore be allowed too."

**Field reference.**

- *Inkscape.* Object > Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical, with the keys H and V in every tool; the selection is flipped about the centre of its bounding box. Two buttons for the same sit in the Selector tool's bar. A flip leaves a rectangle a `rect` with a `transform` in the SVG and bakes nothing. The customer reports that dragging a scale handle past the opposite side flips the object; the sources found in the product owner's search for this spec confirm the flip commands and keys only, so the drag and the sign of the typed fields are to be compared at the demo.
- *Illustrator.* Reflect tool and Object > Transform > Reflect, with an axis and a copy button; the Transform panel's W and H take a negative number. Affinity: Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical in the toolbar and the Transform panel, about the selection's box.
- *LightBurn.* Mirror Horizontal and Mirror Vertical buttons in the toolbar, about the selection; matters for laser work because a design for the back of a material, or an engraving seen through glass, has to be mirrored. Its Numeric Edits toolbar has width and height boxes with percentages; whether a negative value is accepted there is not documented in the sources found.
- *Where we do better.* The two buttons sit in the Select bar where the hand already is, and they work on a polygon, a star, a rounded rectangle and a compound path without converting any of them: a mirrored primitive stays the primitive it was, with its numbers, because a reflection of a rectangle, an ellipse, a polygon or a star is again one. A drag through the opposite edge, a typed negative size and the flip button give the same result for the same intent, one rule for all three. Nothing tells the maker off at zero: a size that would land on 0 becomes 0.01 mm, with a notice, so the shape can always be pulled apart again.

## What exists today

Facts from the specs and from the code on `main` (2026-10-14).

- **A resize clamps at zero.** `resize_local_box` in `transform_math.rs` (used by the single object and, through `group_map`, by every group gesture) floors the new width and height with `max(0.0)`; a drag past the opposite edge stays at factor 0 until the pointer comes back (`0005` criterion 13, `0019` criterion 22, decision D5). The corner radius and stroke factor `stroke_or_radius_factor` also clamp a negative factor to 0.
- **The resize modes that exist:** a corner handle (free), an edge handle (one axis), Ctrl on a corner (one factor from the dominant axis of the drag), Shift on any handle (the box centre is the fixed point; Shift and Ctrl combine), the double-click on a handle (the size chip, fixed point of that handle, Shift at the second press = centre), and the key S (the size chip about the centre). **There is no Alt mode.** In this app Shift means "from the centre" and Ctrl means "keep the proportions", as in Inkscape. The group box of a multi-selection works in the document axes; a single object works in its own oriented box (rotated objects scale along their own axes).
- **The typed size** is the size chip with W and H in millimetres (`transform_entry.rs`, `group_entry.rs`). Both refuse a value of 0 or less with "Must be above 0" (`InvalidReason::NotPositive`). There is **no scale-percent field and no W/H field in the Select bar** and no percent anywhere in a transform entry: the bar holds the two switches, the kind groups and "Object to path".
- **Stretch and conversion** (`0047`): a scale with `|sx - sy|` above 1e-9 is a stretch and converts a polygon or star (alone or in a selection) and, in a multi-selection, a rectangle or ellipse that is not aligned; a uniform scale never converts. `is_stretch(sx, sy)` compares the signed factors.
- **Primitives store positive lengths and a rotation.** A rectangle has bounds and four corner radii (`0013`), an ellipse rx and ry, a polygon or star a frame with a first-vertex angle, and every primitive a `rotation`. A path stores anchors, handle vectors and a `rotation` register that orients its box (`0019` criterion 20). No object kind stores a scale or a matrix, except the clone of `0049` (not built).
- **Switches.** "Scale stroke width" and "Scale corner radius" are read at the press, off at program start; stroke width follows √(sx·sy), floored at 0.01 mm (`0005` criteria 8, 9, 26, 31; `0019` criterion 24).
- **Selection.** A hidden or locked object cannot be selected (`0050` criterion 14); a group or a "Select contents" selection carries its locked descendants along (`0050` criterion 21).
- **History.** One gesture is one step with an operation name (`history_names.rs`: "Resize", "Rotate", "Skew", "Move"); there is no Flip name. The History tab and Ctrl+Z come from `0020`.
- **Keys.** H and V are free letters. Plain letters are gated by `0010` ("Keyboard concept"): no Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, no drag in flight, no text field or chip open. Shift+Ctrl+H is the History tab.
- **Select bar** (`docs/design-system.md`, row "Select bar layout"): the settings group (two switches, always shown, never disabled) comes first, then the kind groups, then "Object to path"; groups wrap whole; a control that acts on a kind shows only when the selection holds that kind; at 800 x 600 with the panel open the switches fit one row.

## Terms

- **Axis of a gesture.** The document axes for a flip button and for every gesture on a group box (a multi-selection, a group). The object's own box axes for a resize of one object (`0005` criterion 18, `0012`).
- **Signed factor.** The scale factor of an axis, positive when the dragged side stays on its side of the fixed point and negative when it is on the other side. A drag of the E handle from x = 30 to x = -10 about the fixed edge x = 0 has sx = -1/3.
- **Mirror** (flip) **about the vertical axis** swaps left and right: the horizontal flip (sx = -1, sy = 1 about the box centre x). **About the horizontal axis** swaps top and bottom: the vertical flip.
- **True bounding box (TBB)** and **group box**: as in `0047` Terms and `0019` criterion 1. The TBB of one object and of a selection of several are computed by the same function, and a single object is not special (`0047` criterion 48).
- **Stretch**, redefined: a scale whose **magnitudes** differ, `| |sx| - |sy| | > 1e-9 * max(|sx|, |sy|)`. A scale with `|sx| = |sy|` is uniform even if one factor is negative, so a mirror never converts anything.
- Lengths are compared with the geometric tolerance of the earlier specs (1e-9 mm for stored values, 1e-6 mm for drawn outlines), angles with 1e-9 rad.

## Changes to accepted behaviour

The customer asked for these; the owning specs are corrected in the PR that builds this slice (the lead updates them at acceptance, as with `0019` and `0047`).

## Acceptance criteria

### Part A: the flip buttons

1. Given the Select tool, then the Select bar carries a group "Flip" of two icon-only buttons, 28 px high with a 24 px minimum target, directly after the settings group (the two switches) and before the kind groups, on the first row at 800 x 600 with the panel open (the ux-engineer measures it; if a row is too narrow the group wraps whole, as every group does, and is never hidden). The group is **always shown while the Select tool is active, in every selection state, nothing selected included**, and both buttons are **disabled while nothing is selected** (never hidden; a disabled button still shows its tooltip). They have no text label. Accessible names: "Flip horizontally" (mirrors left to right) and "Flip vertically" (mirrors top to bottom). Tooltips: "Flip horizontally: mirror the selection left to right about the middle of its box" and "Flip vertically: mirror the selection top to bottom about the middle of its box" (with " (H)" and " (V)" appended if criterion 4 stands). The glyphs are the ux-engineer's; each shows the axis it mirrors about, so the two are told apart without the tooltip. This is a deliberate exception to "a bar never carries disabled groups" of the design system for the empty selection only; a selection never disables the group.
2. Given a selection of one object or several, any mix of kinds, when the maker clicks "Flip horizontally", then every selected object is mirrored about the vertical line through the centre of the selection's TBB (the group box of `0019` criterion 1; for one object its own TBB), in one commit, with the History operation name "Flip horizontally" ("Flip vertically" for the other button), and the selection stays (same objects, same ids). "Flip vertically" mirrors about the horizontal line through the same centre. The TBB of the selection after the flip equals the TBB before, within the geometric tolerance (a mirror about the centre of a box keeps the box). A polite live-region announcement says "Flipped horizontally" (resp. vertically). Example: a rectangle A from (0, 0) to (10, 10) and a rectangle B from (20, 0) to (30, 10) with a 2 mm radius at its top-left corner; the group box is (0, 0) to (30, 10), centre x = 15. After "Flip horizontally": A is from (20, 0) to (30, 10), B is from (0, 0) to (10, 10) and its 2 mm radius is at its top-right corner. Each object is mirrored about the selection's centre, not about its own centre.
3. Given a selection whose flip would change nothing (every object's result equals its start within the tolerances of `0009` criterion 12: a circle at any rotation flipped either way, a horizontal straight line flipped vertically, a symmetric path), when the maker clicks a flip button, then nothing is written and no history step is made, and the canvas notice slot shows "The selection looks the same flipped. Nothing changed." The button is not disabled in advance (the maker should not have to guess why).
4. (Proposal) Given the Select tool with at least one object selected, then the key H flips horizontally and V flips vertically, exactly as the buttons do; both are gated by the keyboard concept of `0010` (ignored with Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, during a drag, in a text field or an open chip, with Space held). With nothing selected the hint "Select an object first" shows, as for M and K. In every other tool H and V do nothing. (Inkscape: H and V in all tools; here the Node tool's selection is nodes, and a flip of the object under it would surprise.) Shift+Ctrl+H stays the History tab.
5. Given a chip (move, size, angle, skew) open, when the maker clicks a flip button, then the chip closes without writing, as a press elsewhere does (`0010` criterion 25), and the click flips. A flip button is not reachable during a drag.

### Part B: scaling over to the other side

6. Given a selected object or a multi-selection and a **free corner drag** (no modifier), when the pointer crosses the vertical line through the fixed corner, then the x factor is negative from there on, the dragged corner follows the pointer 1:1, and the objects are mirrored about the vertical line through the fixed corner; crossing the horizontal line does the same for y; crossing both is a half turn (sx and sy negative). There is no wall, no snap and no dead band at 0: the object is drawn as the pointer gives it in every frame, except that an axis extent that would be exactly 0 (criterion 15) is drawn as 0.01 mm. Example: a closed path with corner nodes (0, 0), (30, 0), (0, 10); the SE handle at (30, 10) dragged to (-30, -10): the fixed point is the NW corner (0, 0), sx = -1, sy = -1, the nodes become (0, 0), (-30, 0), (0, -10). Dragged to (-30, 20) instead: sx = -1, sy = 2, the nodes become (0, 0), (-30, 0), (0, 20).
7. Given an **edge handle** drag, when the pointer crosses the opposite edge, then only the axis perpendicular to that edge gets a negative factor, the other axis is untouched, and the opposite edge is the fixed line. Example: a 30 x 10 rectangle from (0, 0) to (30, 10), the E handle dragged from x = 30 to x = -10: sx = -1/3, the result is a rectangle from (-10, 0) to (0, 10), still a rectangle, height unchanged.
8. Given **Ctrl** on a corner handle (keep the proportions), then the common magnitude comes from the dominant axis of the drag as today (`0005` criterion 5), and **each axis keeps the sign the free drag would give it**: the proportions are kept while the maker can still flip one axis or both by where the pointer is. A free factor of exactly 0 counts as positive. Example: the same path, the SE handle dragged with Ctrl from (30, 10) to (-15, 2): the pointer's free factors are sx = -0.5 and sy = 0.2, the dominant axis is x, so the common magnitude is 0.5 with sx negative and sy positive: the nodes become (0, 0), (-15, 0), (0, 5). Ctrl on an edge handle has no effect, as today.
9. Given **Shift** (the box centre is the fixed point), then both sides of the touched axis move symmetrically about the centre and the factor of that axis is negative when the pointer is on the opposite side of the centre from the handle; the result is a mirror about the centre line of that axis. Shift and Ctrl combine: the common magnitude and the signs are those of criterion 8, about the centre. The modifiers are read at the press (`0008` criterion 14), as today. Example: the 30 x 10 rectangle, the E handle dragged with Shift from x = 30 to x = -10: the centre is x = 15, the signed width is -50, the rectangle is from (-10, 0) to (40, 10).
10. Given any resize drag that has crossed zero, then the preview is the one of `unified-object-editing` (blue outline, committed geometry in its own style) of the objects as they would be committed; it follows the pointer in every frame; Escape drops the drag; a release writes one commit with the label "Resize"; a press and release under the dead zone writes nothing; the pivot marker stays at the fixed point; the live readout of `0005` criterion 14 and `0019` criterion 23 shows the **signed** size with a real minus sign (U+2212) on a negative axis, "−12.5 mm × 8.0 mm", so the maker sees that the object is turned over; a size under 0.1 mm is shown with two decimals ("0.01 mm", "−0.01 mm"). A drag back across zero returns to the original orientation with no residue (the result depends on the pointer position, the press state and, only for a frame that lands on exactly 0, the sign of the frame before; criterion 15).
11. Given the result of a drag that crossed zero, then the selection box, handles and cursors are those of the result, by position: the handle at the left edge is the W handle whatever handle was grabbed, and a new drag of it keeps the opposite edge as the fixed line. For one object the box is drawn on the mirrored object in its own axes (a rotated rectangle keeps its angle after a mirror along its own axis, because the box is symmetric); for a flip of a rotated object about the document axes the box is the mirror image of the box before (criterion 18). Test: after the example of criterion 7 the handle at the left edge of the box (x = -10) is the W handle; dragging it 5 mm to the right gives a rectangle from (-5, 0) to (0, 10).
12. Given a multi-selection or a group, then the resize works in the document axes on the group box and every object takes the same signed factors by criterion 18. Given exactly one rotated object, then the resize works along its own box axes: a rectangle with `rotation` 30° from (0, 0) to (20, 10) before rotation, centre (10, 5), its E handle dragged along its own x axis to 10 mm beyond the W side gives sx = -0.5, `rotation` stays 30°, and the centre moves to (10, 5) - 15 · (cos 30°, sin 30°) = (-2.99, -2.50) (to 0.01 mm). The pointer is mapped into the box's own frame as today (`0005` criterion 18).
13. Given a factor that would put any coordinate of any selected object beyond ±1e7 mm, a non-finite pointer, or any result that is not sane, then the whole resolution is no change, all or nothing, nothing converted and nothing moved (`0019` criterion 22 second sentence, unchanged). A start extent of 0 on an axis (a straight line's thickness) has no factor on that axis, as today (`editable` false, `safe_factor` 1.0).

### Part C: typed values

14. Given the size chip (double-click on a resize handle, or the key S), then the W and H fields accept any finite number, positive, negative or zero (zero is set to 0.01 mm by criterion 15), with "." or "," and the real minus sign U+2212, as `parse_entry_number` reads them today. The typed number is the **signed size on the fixed point's other side**: the signed factor is the typed value divided by the current size of that axis, applied about the chip's fixed point (the opposite side of the grabbed handle, or the centre for the key S and for Shift at the second press). A negative number mirrors; its magnitude is the new size. An unedited field means "no change on that axis". Enter commits with the label "Resize". Examples: the 30 x 10 rectangle at (0, 0), the E handle's chip opened by double-click (fixed edge x = 0), W typed -30: the rectangle is from (-30, 0) to (0, 10), a mirror about x = 0. The key S on the closed path (0, 0), (30, 0), (0, 10) with W typed -30: the centre line x = 15 is the fixed line, the nodes become (30, 0), (0, 0), (30, 10). W typed -30 and H typed -10 on the same: a half turn about the centre. The prefill of a field is always the current positive size; a reopened chip after the mirror shows 30.0 again, not -30.0 (the size has no sign, the factor does).
15. **A size never lands on 0 (customer decision ZERO-RECOVER).** Given a resize whose resolved extent on an axis is exactly 0 (its magnitude is under 0.0005 mm, half the 0.001 mm tolerance of the app; the document has no stored 0.001 mm grid, the threshold is a constant of the rule), by a typed 0 in W or H or by a drag frame (or release) that lands there, then that axis is set to **0.01 mm** instead (a value on the 0.001 mm grid), with the sign of the last step: for a drag, the sign the extent had in the previous preview frame whose extent was not 0 (positive if there was none); for a typed 0, positive. It is not a refusal and not a flat collapse: the object keeps a size, its handle stays grabbable and the shape can be pulled apart again. The rule applies to the extent of the box of the gesture (the object's own box for one object, the group box for a multi-selection and a group), in every mode: with Ctrl the dominant axis gets 0.01 mm and the other follows by the common factor; with Shift the symmetric extent is 0.01 mm about the centre; an axis whose start extent is 0 has no factor and is not touched (criterion 13), and neither is an axis whose start extent is already under 0.0005 mm; the proportional partner under Ctrl is not rewritten either, even if its extent falls under 0.0005 mm. The flip button (factors of magnitude 1) is not a resize and never triggers the rule. A frame that lands on 0.01 mm is previewed, and a release there is committed, as the object at that size, in one commit labelled "Resize". Neither a drag nor a typed value ever writes an extent under 0.0005 mm on a driven axis whose start extent was 0.0005 mm or more. Notice (Action notice style, 3 s, `role="status"`, canvas notice slot, after the release or the Enter; texts within the 70-character limit): "Width set to 0.01 mm. A zero size would flatten the shape for good." (67 characters), "Height set to 0.01 mm. A zero size would flatten the shape for good." (68), and for both axes "Width and height set to 0.01 mm. Zero would flatten the shape." (62). No notice appears for a frame that lands on 0 during a drag that then moves on and is released elsewhere. The old message "Must be above 0" no longer exists. Pulling a 0.01 mm axis apart again works like any other: an object 0.01 mm wide with its E handle dragged 20 mm to the right is 20.01 mm wide, and the pull can be repeated through 0 and back any number of times. Examples: the 30 x 10 rectangle from (0, 0), the E handle's chip, W typed 0: the rectangle is from (0, 0) to (0.01, 10) and the notice shows. The same rectangle, the E handle dragged to x = 5 (previous frame, sx > 0), then in the next frame to x = 0 exactly: the preview is 0.01 mm wide on the right of x = 0; moved on to x = -4 it is 4 mm wide on the left, mirrored. The sign of a frame that lands on 0 after coming from the left (negative) side is negative: the rectangle is from (-0.01, 0) to (0, 10).
16. Removed 2026-10-14 (customer decision PERCENT A: millimetres only). The number is kept so that references stay valid. No W or H field takes a "%" suffix; the mirror is a flip button or a negative size in mm.
17. Given the entry, then the remaining refusals are unchanged: text that is not a number is refused with "Enter a number"; a magnitude above the coordinate limit or a result beyond ±1e7 mm on any edge is refused with "Too large"; both keep the chip open and write nothing. A signed value equal to the current size (+30 on a 30 mm side) writes nothing, as today; the same magnitude with a minus sign is a change. The linked fields (Ctrl at the second press) carry the typed sign to the other field: W typed -30 on a 30 x 10 box shows H -10.0 and means the half turn.

### Part D: what a negative scale does to each kind

18. **The general rule.** Given a map with signed factors (sx, sy) about a fixed point, in the axes of the gesture, then each object is first scaled by the magnitudes (|sx|, |sy|) by the rules that already exist (`0019` criterion 20 as changed by `0047`) and then mirrored once per negative factor about the same fixed point. Whether the gesture is a stretch is decided on the magnitudes (Terms). Therefore: a mirror, a half turn and a uniform scale with any signs convert nothing; a stretch converts exactly the objects that a stretch with positive factors converts, no more. A flip button is the map (-1, 1) or (1, -1) about the TBB centre, in the document axes. The outline drawn after the map is the exact image of the outline before under the signed map, within 1e-6 mm, for every object whose rule is in this part, except a rounded rectangle whose radius does not follow (as `0019` criterion 20 states).
19. **Path and compound path.** Every anchor point is mapped, every handle vector is multiplied by (sx, sy) per component (so a negative factor reverses that component), the node kinds (Corner, Symmetric, Asymmetric) are unchanged (collinearity and length ratios of the two handles survive a reflection), the order of the anchors, the anchor ids, the open or closed state and the start node are unchanged: **the direction of the path is not reversed** (the travel direction as seen on the screen is reversed by a single mirror; a closed outline that ran clockwise now runs anticlockwise, as in Inkscape; nothing in the file or in an output yet depends on it, see Out of scope). A compound path keeps its outlines, order and holes: holes stay holes because the fill rule is the same for a mirrored outline set (`0016` criterion 31). The `rotation` register of a path (the orientation of its box) becomes the mirror image: for a flip about the document axes θ becomes -θ; for a mirror along the object's own box axis it is unchanged (the box is symmetric); for a half turn it is unchanged. Test: a ring (outer square, square hole, off-centre inside the square) flipped horizontally: the hole is still a hole and the hole's centre is where the mirror puts it.
20. **Rectangle.** Given a rectangle, then it stays a rectangle, with positive width and height (the magnitudes' result), its centre mapped, and its four corner radii **at the same physical corners**: the corner that was at the top-left and is at the top-right after a horizontal mirror keeps its radius (the radii are permuted so that each stays at its physical corner: seen on the screen a horizontal mirror swaps left and right, a vertical one top and bottom, a half turn diagonally. In the rectangle's own frame, with `rotation` negated as below, a flip about the document axes always swaps the local left and right radii (horizontal flip) or the local top and bottom radii (vertical flip), whatever the rotation, 90° included; the test is the physical corner, not the local name). Its `rotation` after a flip about the document axes is the negative of what it was (30° becomes -30°, shown as -30° in the angle chip); after a mirror along its own axis or a half turn it is unchanged. Example: a 30 x 10 rectangle from (0, 0) to (30, 10), radius 3 mm at its top-left corner only: "Flip horizontally" gives the same bounds with the 3 mm radius at its top-right corner only; "Flip vertically" gives it at the bottom-left corner only; both give it at the bottom-right.
21. **Ellipse and circle.** Given an ellipse, then it stays an ellipse with rx, ry the magnitudes' result, centre mapped, `rotation` as for the rectangle (negated by a document-axes flip, unchanged by a mirror along its own axis). A circle (rx = ry within tolerance) stays a circle under a mirror and a half turn, and becomes an ellipse with `rotation` 0 only under a stretch, as `0019` criterion 20 says.
22. **Polygon and star.** Given a polygon or a star, then under a mirror, a half turn or a uniform scale with any signs it stays a polygon or star with the same point count, the same inner ratio, the outer radius times the common magnitude, the centre mapped, and the first-vertex angle (and `rotation`) set so that the drawn outline is the exact mirror image; its selection box follows the shown orientation (`0012`). Under a stretch it is converted by `0047` and mapped as a path. Example: a triangle (3 points), outer radius 5, centre (25, 5), first vertex pointing right, so the vertices are (30, 5), (22.5, 9.33), (22.5, 0.67): its TBB is x from 22.5 to 30, y from 0.67 to 9.33. "Flip horizontally" mirrors about x = 26.25 (the TBB's centre, not the polygon's centre 25): the vertices become (22.5, 5), (30, 9.33), (30, 0.67), the result is a polygon with 3 points and outer radius 5 and its centre is (27.5, 5).
23. **Stroke, markers, dashes, fills.** Given the switches of the Select bar (read at the press), then with "Scale stroke width" on, the stroke width is multiplied by √|sx·sy| (never by 0 because a factor is negative) and floored at 0.01 mm; the corner radii likewise with "Scale corner radius"; with a switch off the stored value is not rewritten. A flip button (magnitudes 1) never changes a stroke width or a radius. Dash lengths, dash phase, colours, opacity, fill rule and fills are never written (a solid colour has no direction; gradients no longer exist, `0017`). A start or end marker and the markers along the path (`0018`) stay on the same nodes and follow the mirrored tangent: a start arrow stays at the start node and points along the mirrored path. Text and raster objects do not exist yet; each will say in its own spec what a flip does to it (criterion 24's rule is the default: mirror the drawn result).
24. **Groups, layers, locked and hidden.** Given a selected group (`0023`) or a "Select contents" selection (`0050`), then a flip or a scale over zero acts on all descendants, locked ones included, about the TBB of the selected node or nodes; the group has no transform of its own. Given a selection made of several objects, then a hidden or locked object is never in it and so is never touched (`0050` criterion 14). The flip buttons depend only on whether something is selected. A nested group is mirrored as its descendants, each by the rules of this part.
25. **Clones** (applies when `0049` is built). Given a selection with a clone, then the clone's matrix becomes G·M with G the signed map when no object of its chain is selected with it, and G·M·G⁻¹ when its original is (`0049` criteria 15 and 16; G is always invertible, because a factor is never 0, criterion 15); a reflection makes the determinant of M negative, which is valid and is drawn, hit-tested and measured by the resolved outline. Test: an origin triangle (0, 0), (10, 0), (0, 10) and a clone with M = translate (30, 0), covering (30, 0), (40, 0), (30, 10); the clone alone selected and flipped horizontally: its box is x from 30 to 40, centre 35, M becomes (a, b, c, d, e, f) = (-1, 0, 0, 1, 40, 0), the clone covers (40, 0), (30, 0), (40, 10) and the origin is untouched. Stroke width, dash and marker size of a clone are not scaled (`0049` criterion 9). No scale of this feature has a factor of 0 (criterion 15), so none of its gestures unlinks a clone; the unlink of `0049` criterion 16 stays for a chain whose determinant is below 1e-9 for another reason.
26. **File.** Given a project in which objects were flipped, scaled over zero or typed negative, when the maker saves, closes and reopens it, then every object is as before closing: no stored length, radius or ratio is negative, no scale or matrix is written for any object of an existing kind, no object changes kind except by a stretch's conversion, and `format_version` does not change (a mirror of an existing kind is written with the fields of that kind). A file written by this version opens in the previous version without loss.

### Part E: budget, numbers, undo

27. Given a selection of 5,000 objects (2,500 paths with 20 nodes, 1,250 rectangles, 1,250 stars, half of the primitives turned by 30°), in a native release build (`#[ignore]` benchmarks on the build host, as `0019` criteria 48 and 49): computing the group box takes under 10 ms; one preview step of a resize that has crossed zero takes under 25 ms; the commit of a flip and the commit of a drag released beyond zero each finish within 2.5 s and take at most 1.1 times the commit of a plain move of the same selection, measured in the same run; Escape writes nothing. From the release until the commit is presented the cursor is `wait` and the preview stays unchanged (`0019` criterion 49). The browser (WASM) times are reported in the PR description, not gated.
28. Given the arithmetic, then these hold as automated tests in `curvyo-ui-core`: (a) flipping horizontally twice, or vertically twice, gives every object back within 1e-9 mm and 1e-12 rad, for every kind and for a selection of mixed kinds, at coordinates within ±1e5 mm; (b) a horizontal flip followed by a vertical flip equals a rotation by 180° about the TBB centre, outline for outline within 1e-6 mm; (c) the TBB of any selection is unchanged by either flip within the tolerance; (d) a scale by (-s, -s) about a point equals a rotation by 180° about it followed by a scale by (s, s); (e) a drag released at a point, the same size typed into the chip with the same fixed point, and (for an unrotated selection and the centre as the fixed point) the flip button give snapshots equal within the tolerance for the same signed map; (f) the same input gives the same output byte for byte: independent of the order in which the objects were selected, with every id, anchor id and anchor order unchanged; (g) a drag that crosses zero and returns gives back the start; (h) zero recovery (criterion 15): a typed 0 gives exactly +0.01 mm on that axis, a resolved extent of 0 and of 0.0004 mm give 0.01 mm, an extent of 0.0006 mm stays as it is, a drag frame landing on 0 after a positive frame gives +0.01 mm and after a negative frame -0.01 mm, a first frame landing on 0 gives +0.01 mm, the Ctrl, Shift and Ctrl+Shift variants give 0.01 mm on the dominant or symmetric axis with the proportions kept, and no gesture writes an extent of 0 on an axis whose start extent was above 0; (i) pull-apart: from an object 0.01 mm wide, an E-handle drag of 20 mm gives 20.01 mm, and three repeated drags through the fixed line and back each give the expected size; (j) determinism: the same event sequence (including a frame on 0) gives the same bytes twice, and preview and commit agree. These cover each kind of Part D (path, compound path, rectangle with four different radii, ellipse, circle, polygon, star) at least once.
29. Given `0020`, then a flip is one step named "Flip horizontally" or "Flip vertically" and a resize released beyond zero or typed negative is one step named "Resize"; Ctrl+Z brings back the exact previous objects with all parameters (width, height, four radii, rx, ry, point count, ratio, `rotation`, anchors, handles); redo does it again; a flip button pressed with a chip open writes only the flip. No other step is made by a conversion (`0047` criterion 4 applies unchanged).
30. Given `docs/design-system.md`, then the PR adds the rows for the Flip group (placement, glyph rule, disabled state, tooltip text), the signed size readout and the notice for a size set to 0.01 mm, and the ux-engineer lists the rows of "Select bar layout" that it changes.

## Out of scope

- A flip of each object about its own centre (Illustrator's "Transform each"); the flip is about the selection. A modifier for it is a possible later request.
- A mirror axis the maker places or picks (Illustrator's Reflect tool, LightBurn's mirror about a guide) and a copy-while-mirroring (Illustrator's Copy button). The Ctrl copy of a move and Duplicate stay as they are; "duplicate, then flip" is two actions.
- A flip of nodes in the Node tool, a flip of a selection of nodes, and the parameter handles of `0009` and the creation drags of the shape tools: they keep their limits at zero.
- Reversing the direction of a path (a "Reverse path" command). Nothing in the file or the app depends on the direction until the laser, plotter and CNC outputs (`0029` and after), where the climb or conventional direction of a mirrored outline is decided by the machine slice; a mirrored outline keeps its anchor order. Also flipping the canvas view.
- A percent unit: no "%" suffix in the size fields, no scale-percent field and no W/H field in the Select bar (customer decision PERCENT A).
- A way to make an axis exactly 0 mm wide or high (a flat shape) by scaling; a straight line's own zero extent is untouched.
- Snapping of the pointer to the mirror position (a "snap to the exact mirror size"), a snap at 0, and any guide while dragging through.
- Text, raster images and traced bitmaps (not built); each defines its flip in its own spec.
- The rotate and skew handles and chips: a negative angle is allowed there already.
- A Mirror setting for a machine job ("mirror for the back of the material"): a job property of the output slices.

## Decided by the product owner

Each is vetoable at the demo; none changes what the customer asked for.

- **D1. The flip centre is the TBB centre of the whole selection.** Group box semantics, as for every other group gesture (`0019`, `0047` criterion 48); Inkscape, Affinity and LightBurn flip the selection as one. Not the oriented box: a flip keeps the TBB, which is easy to test and to see.
- **D2. Flips are in the document axes, scaling over zero is in the axes of the gesture.** The buttons mean left/right and top/bottom of the screen; a drag of an own-axis handle of a rotated object mirrors along that axis (criterion 12). Both are the existing frames of the earlier specs.
- **D3. A mirrored primitive stays a primitive.** The reflection of a rectangle, ellipse, polygon or star is one of the same kind, so nothing is converted by a mirror, a half turn or a uniform scale with any signs. Conversion is still only for a true stretch, decided on the magnitudes.
- **D4. Path direction is kept, not reversed.** As in Inkscape; reversing would change the start/end meaning of markers and of an open path's first node under the maker's hand.
- **D5. Ctrl keeps the proportions and the signs follow the pointer** (criterion 8), so the maker can flip one axis of a proportional resize. The request spoke of "Shift keeps the ratio"; in this app Ctrl keeps it and Shift is the centre, so the criteria use the app's bindings (the customer can say if the request meant something else).
- **D6. A flip with no effect writes nothing and says so** (criterion 3); the button is never disabled for that reason.
- **D7. The Flip group is always shown, disabled only while nothing is selected** (customer: never hidden). It sits after the settings group so the first row does not move for any selection.
- **D8. Typed sizes are signed factors about the chip's fixed point**, so the same typed value means the same as the same drag (criterion 14); the chip's prefill is always the positive size.
- **D9. H and V are a Proposal and only in the Select tool.**
- **D10. No format change, no new dependency, no new crate.**

- **Customer decisions of 2026-10-14.**
  - **PERCENT: A, millimetres only.** No "%" suffix and no percent field (criterion 16 removed). The mirror is a flip button or a negative size in mm.
  - **ZERO-TYPED: superseded.** Its option A (a typed 0 collapses the shape, with a notice) was the product owner's default; the customer replaced it the same day by ZERO-RECOVER.
  - **ZERO-RECOVER: C.** An axis that would land on exactly 0, by typing 0 or by a drag that ends exactly there, is set to 0.01 mm instead, with the sign of the last step (drag) or positive (typed 0), plus a notice. No refusal and no flat collapse (criterion 15). Product owner's reading, vetoable: "exactly 0" means a resolved extent under 0.0005 mm (it rounds to 0 on the 0.001 mm grid), so a floating-point near-zero cannot make a degenerate shape; "the last step" is the previous preview frame with a non-zero extent; the rule applies to the extent of the gesture's box, and under Ctrl to the dominant axis.

None. Both questions of the first draft are decided (see the customer decisions above).

## UX notes

By the ux-engineer, 2026-10-14, checked against `docs/design-system.md` (rows "Select bar layout", "Select bar", "Link corners toggle", "Transform entry chip", "Live transform readout", "Transform handle layout", "Group handle layout", "Modifier badge", "Action notice", "Canvas notice slot", "Hidden, not disabled", "Command glyphs", "Keyboard shortcuts established so far"). They add nothing to the scope and change no PO decision. The PR writes the design-system rows listed at the end (criterion 30).

### Flip buttons (criteria 1 to 5)

- **Control.** Two 28 x 28 px icon buttons, `rounded-md`, no label, in one `role="group"` named "Flip". They are plain command buttons, not toggles: no `aria-pressed`, no active fill. Glyph 16 px, 1.5 px absolute stroke, `currentColor` (`--toolbar-icon`). Hover `--editor-accent-hover`; focus-visible 2 px `--editor-accent` ring with 1 px `--toolbar-bg` offset (as "Link corners toggle"). Hit target 28 px, above the 24 px minimum. Accessible names as in criterion 1; the shortcut is not part of the name.
- **Glyphs.** Custom, in the family of the row "Command glyphs": 16 x 16 viewBox, at most three shapes, no colour. Horizontal flip: a dashed vertical axis (x = 8, 2.5 on / 2 off, three dashes) with a right-angled triangle on each side, the left one outlined and the right one filled, the legs parallel to the axis 2 units from it, the hypotenuses facing away. Vertical flip: the same drawing turned 90 degrees (not mirrored), so the dashed axis is horizontal and the outlined triangle is on top. The axis orientation is the main cue; outlined against filled says "original and image" and keeps the two readable when the dashes blur. Lucide `FlipHorizontal2` and `FlipVertical2` are the fallback only if the custom pair fails the review test: at 1x and 2x, both themes, glyphs side by side without tooltip, the two are told apart at a glance. Do not use `ArrowLeftRight` or `ArrowUpDown` (they read as "move" or "sort").
- **Placement.** The Flip group follows the settings group, then the kind groups (D7). A 1 px 20 px 25 % divider and 12 px gap on each side where both neighbours are on the same row; 4 px between the two buttons. The group wraps whole and is never hidden. **Expected at 800 x 600, panel open:** the measured row is 356 px and the two switches already use about 345 px of it (`px-1` labels, 4 px apart), so the group (about 12 + 1 + 12 + 60 px) will **not** fit on row 1; it wraps whole to row 2 and the row-1 switches keep their y. That means one row less room than today with nothing selected: the bar is 2 rows (72 px) instead of 1, and every count in the row "Select bar layout" grows by the Flip row only when the kind groups share it (a rectangle selection stays at 3 rows if Flip and "Object to path" fit together on row 2; the implementer measures and records it). Criterion 1 allows this ("wraps whole"); I do not ask for a change. The group sits on row 1 from a canvas width of about 460 px for the bar.
- **Disabled state (nothing selected).** `aria-disabled="true"`, not the `disabled` attribute: the buttons stay in the Tab order and keep their tooltip on hover and on focus; a click or Enter does nothing; glyph at 40 % opacity, no hover fill. This is the second recorded exception to "Hidden, not disabled" (the first is the Style tab of `0043`), for the same reason: a bar whose buttons come and go moves under the hand. The disabled tooltip adds a third muted line "Select an object first."
- **Tooltip.** Radix `Tooltip`, 400 ms, `side="bottom"`, max width 260 px. Line 1 (semibold): "Flip horizontally (H)" or "Flip vertically (V)" (the key only while the Proposal stands). Line 2 (`--panel-muted-fg`): "Mirror the selection left to right about the middle of its box." / "Mirror the selection top to bottom about the middle of its box." The words are those of criterion 1, split into name and rule as in the row "Rail tooltip and notice placement". The key in the name line is the one place a shortcut is written on the bar.
- **Keys H and V (criterion 4, Proposal).** Conflict check against the table "Keyboard shortcuts established so far", the tool letters (S, B, N, P, R, E, `*`, O), the selection entries (M, R, S, K, Shift+K), `0044` (arrows) and `0050` (Home, End, Page Up, Page Down): H and V are unused, and `0032` already reserves them. Gated like every letter ("Keyboard concept": no Ctrl, Cmd or Alt, no drag, no open chip, no focused text control, no repeat; one flip per press, so holding the key does not flip back and forth). Shift+H and Shift+V stay free. With nothing selected: the 2 s hint "Select an object first" in the canvas notice slot, as for M and K. **Risk to tell the customer:** in Figma, Illustrator and Affinity V is the Select tool and H the Hand tool; a maker coming from those presses V and mirrors the selection. It is one undoable step with a visible result and a live-region announcement, and Inkscape (the app's reference) uses exactly H and V, so I recommend keeping the Proposal. The tooltip carries the key, so it is discoverable; an entry in the shortcut table is added in the PR.
- **Keyboard route.** Tab reaches the group after the two switches (Tab stops: switch, switch, Flip horizontally, Flip vertically, then the kind groups). Space or Enter flips; focus stays on the button (the group is not removed, so no focus is lost), and the selection stays on the canvas. H and V need canvas focus. A click on a button closes an open chip without writing (criterion 5), and the chip's Escape rule is unchanged.
- **Announce and notices.** A flip announces "Flipped horizontally" / "Flipped vertically" in the existing polite live region; no visible toast on success (the canvas changes). The no-op notice (criterion 3) is an Action notice: caused by a button it sits 4 px under that button, right-aligned, caused by H or V it appears in the canvas notice slot. Success style, `role="status"`, 3 s (54 characters, under the 70 limit), text exactly as criterion 3. It is not styled as a refusal: nothing went wrong.

### Dragging through the opposite side (criteria 6 to 13)

- **Handles and cursors follow the result, by position (criterion 11).** Each frame the box, the handles and their cursors are drawn for the preview. The grabbed handle is now on the far side of the fixed point and takes the role (and the cursor) of the handle at that position; the pivot marker and the fixed handle do not move. A corner dragged across one axis therefore changes its cursor from the `nesw` double arrow to the `nwse` one in the same frame (across both axes the double arrow is the same, since NE and SW share it). The cursor image is rotated with the box for a single rotated object, as now. An edge handle keeps its cursor (W and E share the arrow). The grabbed handle stays in its dragging look (solid `--accent`) wherever it has moved.
- **The handle set is frozen for the duration of a drag.** The size tiers of "Transform handle layout" are evaluated at the press and again at the release, never per frame: a box that shrinks to zero width on the way through must not lose the handle under the pointer. After the release the tiers apply to the result (an object 0.01 mm wide keeps corner resize and corner rotate, which never hide, so it can be pulled apart again).
- **Dashed selection box and the inverted case.** The box is always drawn normalised (min to max), so it never turns inside out; it carries no orientation of its own. The mirror is read from the preview (the blue outline of the objects as committed) and from the readout, not from the box. For a rotated single object the box keeps its angle after a mirror along its own axis (symmetric), and is the mirror image after a document-axes flip (criterion 11). A box 0.01 mm wide (criterion 15) is drawn as the dashed line it looks like at normal zoom, with the handles on it; no special marker.
- **Cue that the object is mirrored: the readout.** The live readout (12 px up and right of the pointer) shows the signed size with a real minus on each negative axis, "−12.5 mm × 8.0 mm" (criterion 10). The sign follows the sign of the factor, not the rounded text: "−0.0 mm" is correct just past zero and tells the maker the object has turned over; a size under 0.1 mm shows two decimals, so the 0.01 mm of criterion 15 reads "0.01 mm" or "−0.01 mm". Added under it, one muted 12 px line while a factor is negative, the same words in the chip (below) and on the announcement: "Mirrored left to right" (sx negative), "Mirrored top to bottom" (sy negative), "Turned 180°" (both). For a single rotated object these words are about its own box axes. If the stretch line of `0047` ("N shapes become paths") is also due, it comes third; the chip is at most three lines and keeps its placement and flip rules. Colour is not used: the minus and the words carry it, in both themes. Reduced motion: nothing animates; the object, box and readout change in the frame the pointer crosses.
- **No snap, no dead band, no extra badge.** As criterion 6. The modifier badge row is unchanged (no badge for Ctrl or Shift on a resize). Escape drops the drag as today.
- **The architect's caution (non-dominant axis under Ctrl, D5).** The sign is discrete, so when the pointer crosses the non-dominant axis' line the object pops across the fixed line by its own size in one frame, while the readout gains its minus and its second line in the same frame. That is the specified behaviour (the result depends only on the pointer, and a drag back returns exactly), and the readout line makes the cause readable. To be checked on a real drag in the UX review; if the pop reads as a jump, the fallback is that under Ctrl the signs follow the pointer only on the dominant axis and the other axis keeps its press sign. I do not propose that now (it would change criterion 8).
- **Announcement.** Release announces "Resized" plus the cue sentence when a factor was negative: "Resized. Mirrored left to right." The drag itself stays pointer only; the keyboard route to the same result is the size chip (`S`) with a negative value, or a flip button.

### Typed values (criteria 14 to 17)

- **Chip.** The "Transform entry chip" surface is unchanged: 100 px fields, labels "W" and "H", fixed `mm` suffix, chain glyph when linked. The prefill is the current positive size, selected, so typing replaces it (criterion 14). The field accepts "-" and the real minus U+2212, "." and ",". No field-level validity message for a negative number; "Must be above 0" is gone.
- **Showing that the entry will mirror.** While at least one field holds a negative number the chip shows a muted note line under the fields (12 px `--toolbar-icon` at 80 %, no alert glyph, not `--field-invalid`): "Mirrored left to right" / "Mirrored top to bottom" / "Turned 180°", the same strings as the readout. Linked fields (criterion 17) show both signs, so the note says "Turned 180°". It is `aria-describedby` of the fields and a polite `role="status"` text. The preview outline of the chip, where the size chip previews, shows the mirrored result.
- **Unit (customer decision PERCENT A).** Millimetres only: the suffix cell always shows "mm", a typed "%" is "Enter a number". Discoverability of the mirror: both fields get the accessible description "Millimetres. A minus sign mirrors." (also as the field's `title`). No visible hint text in the chip: it would add a row to a chip that is deliberately small.
- **Errors keep the old look.** "Enter a number" and "Too large": `--field-invalid` 2 px border plus the 12 px message line, chip stays open (existing).
- **Typed 0 and a drag landing on 0 (ZERO-RECOVER, criterion 15).** Enter writes the size 0.01 mm (one step "Resize") and the chip closes. The notice, in the **canvas notice slot** (there is no control to anchor it to; the chip is gone, and after a drag the pointer is gone too), is an Action notice, `role="status"`, 3 s, with the texts of criterion 15 (67, 68 and 62 characters, under the 70 limit). Not a refusal and not an error style. An object 0.01 mm wide keeps its selection box as a line with its handles (see the frozen handle set above).
- **Keyboard route.** `S` opens the chip at the box centre; Tab between W and H; Enter commits; Escape closes. Focus returns to the canvas. Unchanged from the row "Type a size".
