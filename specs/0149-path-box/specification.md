# Path box: turn any closed path (a heart, a shield, a coffin) into a laser-cut box, as a generator

Status: Ready for slice 1 (revision 4, 2026-11-24; `adrs.md` (ADR 0083, Proposed, the architect may still revise it) and `ux-notes.md` revision 1 exist; Fragen 364 to 366 open, all with defaults A (Frage 367 fell away); the UX questions are decided as Proposals (kinks built in slice 1, layout beside the source); slice 2 (curves) stays Draft; depends on `0147` slice 1 being built first)
Priority: Could
Origin: Customer (2026-11-24, translated, an idea added to `0147`): "turn ANY path into a box": a heart, a shield, a coffin. The corners of the path become finger joints, the curves are always slit-bent; "if the radius is too small: own fault, we just do it". Everything marked **(Proposal)** is the product owner's and vetoable at the demo.

## User value

As a maker I want to draw a closed outline, press "Box from path" and get a cut-ready box with that outline (walls, base, lid), so that a heart-shaped gift box, a shield for a prop or a coffin for a tiny pet needs no hand-made finger joints and no hand-drawn slits.

**What other tools do.** Boxes.py and MakerCase build boxes from rectangles or a few fixed polygons; Inkscape's Tabbed Box Maker is rectangular only; Fusion 360 / "Slicer" style add-ins and some LightBurn community scripts make finger-jointed polygons but no bending walls; none turns a drawn outline with mixed corners and curves into one box. We do better: one outline in, a live object (`0141`) out; sharp corners become joints, curves become living hinges, from the same parameter block as the Rounded box.

## Relation to neighbouring specs

- **`0147` rounded-box:** the parameter block, the five parts, the slit rules (no uncut band, kerf plus 0.3 mm slots, Straight, Offset, Wave), per-part thickness fields and the group-like unit (one operation for all parts; Detach part when `0147` slice 3 ships it) are reused unchanged; the Rounded box is the special case "rectangle with four equal arcs". Slice 1 of `0147` (strip, tabs, plates) comes first.
- **`0148` slit-bend-pattern (idea):** the slit pattern along an arbitrary curve is the shared building block; not needed here: the wall piece is slit flat as on the rounded box (ADR 0083 §4), `0148` may reuse `slit_bend.rs` later.
- **`0141` parametric-generators:** another kind through the same contract (ADR 0080); the finger-joint code is extended from 90 degree corners to any corner angle (mitred fingers).
- **`0101` laser operations:** parts share one operation by default, Detach part as in `0147` criterion 15 once its slice 3 exists (not in slice 1).

## Slices

- **Slice 1 (Ready): outlines of straight edges.** Criteria 1 to 5, 8, 10 to 12 (a coffin, a star, a hexagon): corners become finger joints at any angle, base and lid. **A join that turns by less than Corner angle (a kink: a 20-gon, a coffin at Corner angle 90) is built as a bent span with slits**, as the arcs of `0147` (ADR 0083 §3; a loop with no corner left gets the seam and tab windows, criterion 9); slice 1 refuses only an outline with a curved cubic (criterion 1). The customer can try it end to end. **(Proposal, UX question 1 A)**
- **Slice 2 (Draft): curves.** Criteria 6, 7 and 9 for curved cubics (slice 1 already uses them for kinks): curved spans slit-bent, tangent-continuous junctions, smooth closed outlines (circle, ellipse) as one strip: heart, shield. Needs the arc fit (ADR 0083) but no slit pattern along a curve.
- **Later:** a linked (not copied) source path (Frage 364 B), outlines with holes or islands, a different wall height per edge, a sloping wall (cone or taper), curve-aware tab placement options.

## Acceptance criteria (as far as clear)

1. Given a selected single closed object (a path, or a shape such as a rectangle, ellipse or polygon; its outline in the document's units), when the customer presses **Box from path** in the Generators tool (a segment next to Box, Rounded box and Gear; offered as a command on a selected closed outline), then a generated object (kind `path-box`) is built with default parameters, selected, one undo step "Add path box". The button is a command in the Select bar, shown only for one closed outline; the Generators segment is a pick mode (click the outline, or Enter on the selection), UX notes section 1. The outline is the **inner face of the wall** (cover and lid are the outline offset outward by the wall thickness; **Proposal**, ADR 0083). **The flat layout lies to the right of the source's bounding box, Gap apart, top edges aligned; the source stays untouched and clickable** (**Proposal**, UX question 2 B; replaces "over the outline's position"). **Refusals** (a notice ending "Nothing was changed.", no error dialog, nothing built): nothing selected, an open path, a group, several objects or any other ineligible object: "Select one closed outline. Nothing was changed."; a self-intersecting outline: "The outline crosses itself. Nothing was changed."; an outline too small (area below (4 t)^2, t the default Wall thickness): "The outline is too small for a box with 3 mm walls. Nothing was changed."; in slice 1 an outline with a curved cubic: "This outline has curves. Box from path builds straight outlines only so far. Nothing was changed."
2. Given the new object, then its parameters are the block of `0147` criterion 2 (**Inner height** (the same key and label as the Rounded box), the five thickness fields (Wall thickness, Inner base, Cover, Ring and Lid thickness), Kerf compensation, Slit pattern, Wall slits is not offered: curves are always slit-bent, Slit width, Slit spacing and Slit rows automatic, Base inset, Base cover, Lid, Lid seat, Finger hollow, Layout and Gap) **plus** **Corner angle (degrees, a slider, default 25 (Frage 366 A), range 5 to 90)**; no Corner radius (the radii are the path's). The outline itself is stored as a parameter (criterion 8) and has no row; there are no size knobs on the canvas. The Slit rows field stays shown in slice 1 too (a kink has slits). The automatic Slit spacing shows the smallest spacing used.
3. Given the outline, then it is cut into **spans** at its **corners**: a node, or a point of a curve, is a corner when the direction of travel changes there by more than Corner angle (a smooth node, a line meeting a curve tangent-continuously, or a curve's gentle bend is not). A span is **straight** when it stays within 0.1 mm of its chord and **curved** otherwise. Tests (fixtures in `tests/fixtures/`): a coffin (six straight edges) gives six corners and no curved span; a shield (flat top, two straight sides, curved bottom) gives its two top corners and the junctions to the curve as no corner; a heart gives two corners (the top notch and the bottom tip) and two bent spans (its four curved cubics merge tangent-continuously into one span per side, ADR 0083 §10); a circle gives no corner and one curved span.
4. Given a straight span, then its wall piece is a **plain plate** (text or a hole may be placed on it, as the straight wall of `0147`), of the Wall height, joined to the next piece at each corner by **finger joints** of the box of `0141` (finger width 2 times the wall thickness, odd count, one owner per corner, ADR 0082 §1), **cut square, at the corner's angle** (a corner of 60 degrees or 120 degrees as well as 90): off a right angle a finger tip stands proud of or short of the outer face by up to t |cot δ| / 2 (t the wall thickness, δ the turn angle at the corner, 90 degrees for a right angle, as in ADR 0083 §5, so the overshoot is t |cot δ| / 2 and zero at 90 degrees). A test mates every pair of neighbouring pieces at a 60, 90 and 135 degree corner at the given thickness and asserts finger and gap centres agree and every tip lies within t |cot δ| / 2 of the outer face (within the kerf value at 90 degrees, as `0141` criterion 3).
5. Given a corner whose interior angle is below 30 degrees (a spike), then the box is **built anyway** with a status notice naming the wall pieces by their numbers (criterion 12): "The corner between pieces 3 and 4 is very sharp: 22 degrees.", several: "2 corners are very sharp: between pieces 3 and 4, and 6 and 1."; the corner also carries a red cross on the canvas; nothing is refused (customer: "own fault, we just do it"; **Proposal** for the notice).
6. Given a curved span, then its wall piece is **always slit-bent** with the slit pattern of `0147` (criterion 4: parallel to the bend axis, running out through the top or bottom edge alternately, no uncut band, closed slots of kerf plus 0.3 mm), the spacing automatic **from the span's local radius** (0.22 times the radius, so a tight curve gets denser slits than a wide one; a stored 0 means automatic as in `0147`). A **curved span that runs tangent-continuously into a straight span stays one piece** (the strip is not cut at a smooth junction); a solid margin of z + t between the slits and a finger edge (z the finger's depth into the overlap zone; ADR 0083 §5), which is 2 times the wall thickness at 90 degrees and longer at sharper corners. Tests as in `0147` criterion 4 per curved span, and the piece length equals the span's length on the neutral line within 0.1 mm.
7. Given a radius too small to bend (below 2 times the wall thickness, as in a sharp curve of a heart), then the box is **built anyway** with a status notice naming the wall piece by its number: "Piece 2 bends to 2.5 mm; below 6 mm the wall may crack.", several: "Pieces 2 and 5 bend below 6 mm; the wall may crack there." (6 mm is 2 times the Wall thickness); the slits are laid by the same rules and no value is clamped (customer: "if the radius is too small: own fault, we just do it"). No error dialog, no refusal, no change of the path.
8. Given the outline as a parameter, then **the object keeps a copy of the path** (default, Frage 364 A): a list of segments in mm in the object's own frame, stored in the file with the other parameters (`0141` criterion 7: the file opens exactly as stored, the outline survives). The source object stays untouched in the document (Frage 365 A); editing it later does not change the box. Resizing the box is proportional (as `0147` criterion 7) and scales the stored path (the thicknesses, Kerf and Gap stay, so the slit count follows the new length). `format_version`: none of its own (ADR 0083): the outline is a new `Path` parameter, additive; the box takes `format_version` only through Detach part (`0147` slice 3).
9. Given a smooth closed outline (circle, ellipse; no corner), then the wall is **one strip** with its seam at the middle of the back side for a circle (as the exact circle of `0147`); on any other outline with no corner the seam sits at the middle of the longest straight span, never in a bent span (Proposal) and joins the plates by tabs on flat windows of the arc, the seam between two tabs (`0147` criterion 5; the mating test on a curve allows the window sag plus the kerf).
10. Given the plates (inner base, base cover, seat ring, lid), then they are generated to the **outline** (cover and lid to the outline offset by the wall thickness) and join the wall by tab and slot as in `0147` criteria 5, 10, 12 and 13 (with Base inset, Lid seat, Finger hollow; the hollow sits at the middle of the longest straight span, or of the longest span if none is straight, **Proposal**); on a curved span on flat windows (ADR 0082 §5), on a straight span never in the first or last piece. The mating tests of `0147` run on the coffin (slice 1) and the heart (slice 2).
11. Given any parameter change, then the paths regenerate within 100 ms of thread CPU time for the coffin and 300 ms for the heart with 400 slots (Proposal; fixtures); invalid and dependent values are clamped with a status notice, never an error dialog; move, rotate, align, Object to path and operations of `0101` work as for every generated object, and **the box is a group-like unit with Detach part exactly as `0147` criterion 15**.
12. Given the flat layout, then the wall pieces lie in a row (in outline order) with Gap, the plates below in the order of `0147`; nothing overlaps; the wall pieces are numbered in walk order from the outline's start, each number a **badge** on the piece in the layout and one on the inner base at the stretch that piece stands on (a piece shorter than 24 px on screen leaves its badges out; the badges are never cut or exported; **Proposal**, UX notes section 3; replaces "labelled in the Generator block"), so the maker matches pieces to places and the notices can name them.

## Out of scope

- Outlines with holes, islands or several paths; open paths; self-intersecting paths (a notice, criterion 1).
- A linked source path that updates the box when the original is edited (Frage 364 B, later), and re-picking another outline for an existing box (later).
- Different wall heights per edge, tapered or sloping walls, a curved top, a rolling lid, compartments, text on the walls, 3D preview, assembly drawing.
- Choosing per span whether it is bent or jointed (the corner angle slider is the only control) and freehand editing of single spans.
- Material databases, automatic kerf, bend simulation.

## Open questions for the customer (all with defaults)

- **Frage 364 (PATH-SOURCE):** how the box knows its outline. A: it keeps a **copy** of the path (a parameter; later edits of the source do not change the box). B: it **links** the source path and follows its edits (a change of the generator contract, later). **Recommendation and default: A now, B as a follow-up.**
- **Frage 365 (PATH-ORIGINAL):** what happens to the source path when the box is made. A: it stays where it is, untouched. B: it is hidden. C: it is consumed (replaced by the box). **Recommendation and default: A (it is the only copy of the drawing; delete it by hand).**
- **Frage 366 (PATH-ANGLE):** the default Corner angle above which a bend is a joint. A: 25 degrees. B: 45 degrees. C: 15 degrees. **Recommendation and default: A** (a heart's smooth lobes stay curves, a hexagon's 60 degree turns are joints; the slider changes it per box).

## Decisions

Customer 2026-11-24 (translated): corners of the path become finger joints; curves are always slit-bent; a radius that is too small is not refused. Product owner (all **(Proposal)**): Corner angle slider, default 25 degrees; the sharp-corner and too-small-radius notices; the spacing from the local radius; the 2 times thickness margin between slits and finger edges; the hollow's place; the CPU-time budgets; piece numbers. Revision 4 (2026-11-24, slice-1 build, all **Proposal**): criterion 4 uses δ as the turn angle at the corner; the heart has two bent spans (four cubics merge); on an outline with no corner the seam sits at the middle of the longest straight, never in a bent span; a kink's fillet radius is raised so its arc holds 2 slit columns at the minimum gap. Revision 3 (2026-11-24, lead decisions, all **Proposal**, vetoable at the demo): slice 1 builds kinks as bent spans and refuses only curved cubics (UX question 1 A); the layout lies right of the source's bounding box, Gap apart, top edges aligned (question 2 B); criteria 1, 2, 5, 7 and 12 aligned with the UX notes (question 3: the refusal "Select one closed outline" with "Nothing was changed.", piece numbers, "Inner height", reworded notices).
