## User value

As a maker with a laser I want to engrave a photo or a shaded area on wood, slate or anodised metal with the same operation list I already use, choosing how the grey values become laser dots and seeing the result before I burn, so that I do not need a second program for pictures, and I want any shape with a gradient fill (`0124`) to engrave as shades, so that I can find the power or the dither that suits my material.

**What other tools do.** LightBurn has an Image layer mode with a dialog of Threshold, Ordered, Atkinson, Dither, Stucki, Jarvis, Newsprint, Halftone and Grayscale, plus brightness, contrast and gamma in a separate "Adjust image" window; the result is previewed only in its own preview window; there is no gradient object, so power tests are made by importing a bitmap. xTool and Glowforge convert pictures behind a wizard, and the settings cannot be reused on a second picture. Inkscape's laser plug-ins ignore pixels. **We do better:** the picture stays an ordinary object and is assigned to an Image operation like a shape is assigned to Cut (`0101`); its dither mode and adjustments are that operation's own settings and sit in the same unfolded row as Interval and Scan angle (`0111`); Engrave stays the vector hatch; the Operations view and the Toolpath view show the exact dots that burn; a shaded area is any closed shape with a Gradient fill (`0124`), and the job, the time and the file are format-neutral exactly as `0029` made them.

## Words (shown in the UI)

- **Image (operation kind):** the sixth kind, after Cut, Score, Engrave, Engrave + Outline and Cut-out. It is the raster operation: it burns images and Gradient-filled shapes as dots. Marking section, like Engrave. Symbol: the dot-ramp glyph (four columns of 2 x 2 squares falling in density, ink, filled; Proposal; a custom operation glyph, not a Lucide icon, UX notes 2.1; UX delta E8). Cut, Score, Cut-out, Engrave and Engrave + Outline never burn dots; they treat an image as its frame rectangle (criterion 1).
- **Raster item:** a leaf that burns as dots in an Image operation: an image (own, linked clone, member of a clone or of a placed page, criterion 46), or a shape, open path or text object whose fill paint is Gradient (`0124`, criterion 23). Every other leaf is not a raster item (criterion 48).
- **Frame rectangle:** what every kind but Image sees of an image: the rectangle of its frame as it stands on the laser page (a parallelogram when the image is turned or skewed), nothing of its pixels, transparency or opacity (Frage 271 A, strictly). It runs as a rectangle would: Cut and Score trace it, Cut-out cuts it, Engrave hatches it, Engrave + Outline hatches and traces it (criterion 1).
- **Image set:** the material's fourth set per laser beside Cut, Score and Engrave: power, speed, passes and interval, read by an Image operation (Frage 270 B, criterion 2).
- **Dot:** one burn position of a raster item: a square of Interval by Interval on the dot grid (criterion 5). A dot is burned (laser on) or not; in Grey mode a burned dot has its own power.
- **Dot grid:** the dots of one raster item, in rows at the Scan angle, Interval apart.
- **Run:** a stretch of neighbouring burned dots of one row that have the same power; the unit the move list and the Toolpath view use.
- **Dither mode:** how the grey values of a raster item become dots: **Threshold** (a dot burns if the item is darker than the threshold), **Ordered** (a fixed 8 x 8 Bayer pattern), **Floyd-Steinberg**, **Jarvis**, **Atkinson** and **Stucki** (error diffusion), and **Grey** (the dot's power follows its grey value, Frage 229).
- **Image settings:** the group of the unfolded Image row that holds Dither mode, Threshold, Lowest power, Brightness, Contrast, Gamma and Invert (criterion 11). They belong to the Image operation only; no other kind has them. They are operation-only settings in the sense of `0111`: they belong to the operation, never to a material.
- **Gradient fill:** the fill paint of `0124`: a linear or radial shade through any number of colour stops. This story reads its lightness: white burns nothing, black burns fully (criterion 24). It sets no gradient value of its own.
- **Raster engrave:** what an Image operation does with a raster item.

One-line meanings (customer rule: the meaning of a new operation before it is built):

- **Image (new operation kind):** "Burns pictures and shades as rows of dots: darker parts burn, lighter parts do not."
- **Dither mode:** "Chooses how the grey tones become burned and unburned dots."
- **Gradient fill:** "A shade from one colour to another (`0124`); engraved, the darker part burns more."

## Acceptance criteria

"Gradient fill" as in `0124`; "Laser page", "item", "leaf", "Not assigned", "nearest rule", "Cuts last" are as in `0101`; "operation-only settings", "Interval", "Scan angle", "Bidirectional fill", "Overscanning", "Crosshatch" as in `0111`; "job", "burn move", "travel move", "Toolpath view", "checks", "block", "warn", "effective numbers", "output format" as in `0029`; "test grid", "set" as in `0112`; "working image" as in `0058`. All numbers are mm unless a unit is named. One operation kind is added, **Image** (criterion 47); the other five are as before and never burn dots.

### What runs

1. Given an image (own, linked clone, member of a clone or of a placed page; criterion 46) in an Image operation (assigned by the item or by the nearest rule, `0101` criterion 12), then it is raster engraved: the Toolpath view and the file hold its dots (criterion 14), and its frame is not drawn there. Given an image in any other kind, then it counts, strictly, as the **rectangle of its frame** (Frage 271 A; Words) and runs as a rectangle would, never a pixel: **Cut** and **Score** trace the frame as one closed line (`0101` criteria 19, 20); **Cut-out** cuts the frame as the item's one closed contour (`0101` criterion 23); **Engrave** hatches the frame as one filled area at the operation's Interval and Scan angle (`0101` criterion 21); **Engrave + Outline** hatches it and traces the frame (`0101` criterion 22). Transparency, opacity and the pixels' content never change this (a Cut-out that follows the contour of a transparent background is a later idea, Out of scope). An image never produces a warn line in any kind (the earlier UX delta E9, "1 image in Engrave 1 does not burn", is void). Given an image in an Image and in a Cut operation (an item may be in any number, `0101`), then each runs by its own kind: the dots in the Marking section, the frame in the Cutting section. Test: a 40 x 30 mm image in each kind alone: Cut, Score and Cut-out write one closed 40 x 30 mm line; Engrave writes hatch lines that fill 40 x 30 mm and no outline; Engrave + Outline writes the same hatch and the closed line; Image writes dots inside 40 x 30 mm and no line; the Operations view draws the rectangle in the kind's own look in the first five.
2. Given an Image operation, then it has no outline and no Crosshatch: a raster item burns its dots only, with the operation's Power, Speed, Passes and Interval. Its material set is the material's own **Image set** (Frage 270 B; the material pick, "Use material" and "Reset to material" of an Image operation read and write the Image set, never the Engrave set; the Image settings never go into a material). A maker who wants dots and a frame assigns the image to an Image and to a Cut or Score operation (criterion 1); one who wants a hatched rectangle uses Engrave.
3. Given a leaf that is a shape with a Gradient fill (`0124`; rectangle, ellipse, polygon, star, path, compound path, linked clone, an open path closed by its straight fill line, a text object clipped to its glyph outlines) in an Image operation, then it is raster engraved like an image: its dots follow the gradient, clipped to the shape's outline (holes excluded, curves flattened as `0029` criterion 17) and its outline is not drawn. Given the same shape in Engrave or Engrave + Outline, then it runs by the vector rule of `0029` criterion 14 and `0101` criterion 21, exactly as a shape without a gradient: a leaf with a closed outline is hatched as one filled area at the operation's Interval and Scan angle with its own fill rule, its open outlines closed by a straight line, a leaf with only open outlines runs as lines; the fill paint, Gradient included, never counts (`0101` criterion 24), so no dot and no shade is burned there. In Cut, Score and Cut-out only its outline runs. A shape with a Colour or Pattern fill, or with no fill paint, is not a raster item (Frage 228 A; criterion 48).
4. Given a raster item that is a clone, a member of a placed page or a clone's member, or carries a transform (rotation, scale, mirror, skew), then the dots are those of the item as it stands on the laser page (`0029` criterion 17), so a mirrored image engraves mirrored on the bed. The opacity of an image node never changes the dots (Proposal: opacity is a view setting, `0050`).

### The dot grid and the resampling rule

5. Given a raster item, then its dot grid is square: row spacing equals dot size equals the Interval of the operation (`0111` criterion 19; range 0.01 to 2.00). Rows are parallel to the Scan angle (0 degrees: parallel to the bed's X axis, positive angles counter-clockwise on the page, `0111` criterion 20). Dot centres of the first row lie half an Interval inside the item's frame at the Scan angle (the rule of `0029` criterion 14), and a dot is part of the grid when its centre lies inside the item's frame (the frame of an image; the outline of a gradient-filled shape, the closed fill outline of an open path, the glyph outlines of text, curve-flattened as `0029` criterion 17, holes excluded). No dot is ever placed outside the frame.
6. Given the value of a dot, then for an image it is, when the source is finer than the dot grid (several source pixels per dot), the box average of the source at the power-of-two box size nearest below the dot, then a bilinear interpolation between the four nearest pixels of that level, so the average is exact to within a factor of two of the dot's footprint at a fixed cost per dot (Proposal, ADR 0068 §3); and, when the source is coarser (fewer source pixels than dots), a plain bilinear interpolation between the four nearest source pixels, so a small image engraved at a fine Interval stays smooth and not blocky. Grey is the luminance of the pixel's sRGB value (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B on gamma-encoded values, as a fixed integer weighting, Proposal: no colour management, `0022`). A pixel's alpha blends the pixel over white: a fully transparent pixel is white and never burns; a half transparent black pixel reads as mid grey. For a gradient-filled shape the value is not resampled: it is the gradient's colour at the dot's centre (the interpolation of `0124` criterion 5, alpha included), blended over white and converted with the same weighting.
7. Given the source pixels of an image, then they are those of the working image of `0058` (RGBA8, at most 16 megapixels and 8,192 pixels a side); a picture that `0058` reduced (original size larger than working size, `is_reduced`) is engraved from the reduced working image, and the gate warns (criterion 36). Nothing of the source is changed or stored by the engraving.
8. Given the same image, settings and page, then the dots are identical on every platform (native and wasm): the grey value, brightness, contrast, gamma, ordered matrix and all four error diffusions use integer or fixed-point arithmetic only, so no floating-point rounding can differ. Test: the golden dot maps of criterion 40 are byte-identical on both targets.

### Adjustments and dither modes

9. Given the order of the work on a dot's value, then it is: resample (criterion 6), Invert (if on, grey becomes 255 minus grey), Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, then the dither mode. Brightness is -100 to 100 % (whole numbers, default 0), Contrast -100 to 100 % (default 0), Gamma 0.10 to 4.00 (two decimals, default 1.00; above 1 lightens mid tones). Test: Brightness +100 % engraves nothing in any mode; Invert on a black square engraves nothing and on a white square burns the whole square.
10. Given the dither modes, then: **Threshold** burns a dot when its grey is below the Threshold (0 to 100 %, whole numbers, default 50 %; the field is part of the group and applies to Threshold only); **Ordered** compares the grey with the 8 x 8 Bayer matrix at the dot's row and column of the dot grid; **Floyd-Steinberg**, **Jarvis**, **Atkinson** and **Stucki** spread the error of each dot to its not yet decided neighbours with the classic weights (Floyd-Steinberg 7/16, 3/16, 5/16, 1/16; Jarvis the twelve weights over 48; Stucki the twelve weights over 42; Atkinson 1/8 to each of six neighbours, so a quarter of the error is dropped on purpose), row by row from the first row, always in the direction of increasing dot column (not in the direction the head moves, so Bidirectional fill never changes the dots; Proposal), dots outside the frame take no error. **Grey** is Frage 229. A dot of an area that is completely white in any mode is never burned, and in every mode a fully black area burns every dot (Threshold above 0 %, Ordered, the four error diffusions; tested for each). Test: a 16-step grey ramp gives a monotonic number of burned dots in the six dither modes (more darkness, at least as many burned dots) and black burns fully, white burns nothing.
11. Given an unfolded Image row, then, below its numbers and Interval and above the other operation-only settings of `0111` criterion 20 (Bidirectional fill, Scan angle, Overscanning, Z offset; the kind has no Crosshatch), it shows the Image settings group, in this order: Dither mode (the always-visible segmented picker of the font filter "All | Favourites | Cuttable | Single line": a radiogroup of text segments, one selected, no drop-down and no menu; **two fixed rows**, never wrapped by the panel width or the platform font: row 1 Threshold | Ordered | Floyd, row 2 Jarvis | Atkinson | Stucki | Grey; the segment text "Floyd" stands for Floyd-Steinberg, which is its tooltip and accessible name; one Tab stop, the arrow keys move **and select** without wrap, each selection is one undo step "Edit settings"; Floyd-Steinberg by default for a new operation, Proposal; UX E11), Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, Invert (an icon toggle, symbol `Contrast`, tooltip "Invert", off), and **last** Threshold (shown with Threshold only) or Lowest power (shown with Grey only; the mode's own field is the last row of the group, so changing the mode never moves the strip, Brightness, Contrast, Gamma or Invert; the customer found a moving panel restless; replaces UX E1). A damaged `dither` value (criterion 13) shows no pressed segment and the word "Damaged" under the strip; pressing any segment repairs it in one step. The group is always shown in an Image row, whether or not it holds an item yet, and in no other kind's row. Fields are plain number fields with the unit inside ("%", none for Gamma), no sliders, and no captions beyond the field name, as `0111`. Bidirectional fill, Scan angle, Overscanning, Interval and Z offset keep their place and meaning.
12. Given a value in the Image settings outside its range, then it is refused with the range in the field's message and nothing is written; a value with more decimals than the field holds is rounded to its step; a valid change is one undo step "Edit settings" and recomputes the Operations view, the Toolpath view and the checks in the same frame as far as the budget of criterion 37 allows (criterion 20 for the preview). (2026-11-19, ADR 0069 §12:) when the job's new dots do not fit that budget, the edit's frame shows the rows and the total of the last job with the time words "…" and "About …" (and "Burn … · travel …") until the job is finished in the following animation frames; the Toolpath view and the file always use the finished job. Undo restores the setting; no pixel is ever a history step.
13. Given the settings, then they are stored on the operation in the `op_options` register of `0111` as new known keys (no damage rule applies to them): `dither` (`threshold`, `ordered`, `floyd_steinberg`, `jarvis`, `atkinson`, `stucki`, `grey`), `threshold_pct`, `brightness_pct`, `contrast_pct`, `gamma`, `invert`, `lowest_power_pct` (the unit suffix of `overscan_pct` and `scan_angle_deg`, ADR 0069 §7), allowed on the Image kind only (the operation `kind` register of `0101` gets the value `image`, criterion 41); a missing key reads as its default; an unknown value of `dither`, a value out of range or with too many decimals, or a key on another kind, is the damage of `0111` criterion 25 (the operation does not run; one warn line "<n> operations have damaged settings and do not run.", `0029` criterion 33). A material pick, "Use material", "Reset to material" and a material edit never change them (`0111` criterion 23, `0028`). A material's sets per laser are four: Cut, Score, Engrave and Image (Frage 270 B), each of power, speed, passes and interval (the Image set like the Engrave set); the Image settings are not part of any set.

### The move list, time and the file

14. Given a raster item, then the job builder emits its dots as **raster rows**: one entry per row of the dot grid, each holding the row's start point, its direction and the ordered runs of the row (start offset in dots, length in dots, power in %), not one move per dot and not one point per dot. The job stays format-neutral (`0029` criterion 39): a raster row names no G-code, no S value and no feed word; one function builds the Toolpath view and every writer's input (`0029` criterion 9). The ordering inside an Image operation is: the raster items in stacking order, bottom first (Proposal: raster items are not nested shapes, so the inner-first rule of `0029` criterion 22 does not apply to them).
15. Given a row of runs, then the head sweeps the row between its first and last burned dot, with the laser off over unburned dots; a gap of unburned dots longer than 5 mm inside the row is a laser-off travel move at the machine's maximum feed (Proposal for the limit, the architect confirms); a row with no burned dot is skipped and writes nothing; leading and trailing unburned stretches of a row are not travelled. The travel from the end of one row to the start of the next is a laser-off move at the maximum feed; where both rows have the same extent (a solid area) it is the step of one Interval sideways. Test: a half-white image writes no move over the white half.
16. Given Bidirectional fill on (default), then the rows alternate direction and no travel back is added (the step to the next row is at the end where the head stands); off, every row runs in the same direction and the head returns with a laser-off travel move (the rule of `0029` criterion 15). Given Overscanning, then each row is extended on both ends by the stated percentage of the run-out of `0029` criterion 15 with the laser off, and the first burned dot of every row is burned at full feed. An Image operation has no Crosshatch (criterion 2). Given the Scan angle, then the rows are turned as in criterion 5.
17. Given passes (1 to 20), then the whole operation is written once per pass, raster rows included (the rule of `0029` criterion 21), and the dither is not recomputed between passes: the same dots burn each pass.
18. Given the feed, then a raster row is written at the operation's speed with the effective numbers of `0029` criterion 2; the sweep is written at constant feed over the row's span (no acceleration modelled); with a speed above the machine's maximum feed the written feed is the maximum and the gate warns (`0029` criterion 21).
19. Given the GRBL output format and laser mode M4 (dynamic), then a row is written as one `G1` per run with `S` set to the run's power (S = round(power % x S-max / 100), `0029` criterion 25) and `S0` over unburned stretches inside the row, `F` only where it changes; a run of the same power as its neighbour is one `G1`; laser-off travel moves are `G0` as in `0029`. Given laser mode M3 (constant), then every burned run is `M3 S<n>` ... `M5` per run, and unburned gaps are `G0` (as `0029` criterion 25 requires for travel); Grey is then a block (criterion 33). The line count of the file is the number of runs plus the number of rows plus the rows' travel moves; a file is never written with one line per dot. The file stays free of date, path and random numbers (`0029` criterion 24).
20. Given the Toolpath view, then a raster item is drawn from the same rows: burn runs in the Image look (the pale orange of the Engrave look, `0101` criterion 18, no new colour; one device pixel wide at least) and the row travel as thin dashed lines; when the view is zoomed so far out that rows are closer than one device pixel, the item is drawn as its shaded dot area (the dots rendered at the screen's resolution, same data), and the start of the operation keeps its numbered ring and the item gets one arrowhead at its first row (not one per row; Proposal). The Operations view always draws a raster item of an Image operation as its dots in the pale orange look (pale orange dots on a transparent ground, no 45 degree hatch; in Grey each dot at an opacity that follows its power) at a resolution of at most one dot per device pixel, recomputed after an edit of an Image setting within the budget of criterion 37 (d) while the previous dots stay until the new ones are ready (UX E2), and the Toolpath view and the file always use the full dot grid. Nothing is told by colour alone: a raster item in the Operations view carries the Image glyph (the dot ramp) at its corner as `0101` criterion 18 sets. Given an item that has no dots yet (first build: just assigned, the page just opened), then the Operations view shows its frame or outline as a 1 px solid pale-orange line with the Image glyph and nothing else until the dots are ready; after an edit the previous dots stay (UX E13). *As built (2026-11-19, architect review A5):* the Toolpath view draws a raster item at every zoom as its dot picture from the job's rows (the burned dots as stripes and areas in the pale orange, Grey dots by opacity), the same data as the file; no line is drawn over the rows and the row travel has no dashed lines; the operation's numbered ring and one arrowhead at the item's first row are drawn as above.
21. Given the time estimate (`0029` criterion 10), then a raster item's time is the sum of its rows' lengths (first burned dot to last, plus overscan) divided by the feed, times passes, plus the travel of criterion 15 divided by the maximum feed; a raster row of Interval 0.1 mm and 0 dots burned adds nothing. The total and the run time of the operation's row (`0029` criterion 2) include it; "Burn <length> · travel <length>" counts the row lengths as burn and the row steps and long gaps as travel. Test: a 100 mm x 10 mm solid black square at Interval 0.1 mm (100 rows of 100 mm, 99 sideways steps of 0.1 mm = 9.9 mm), speed 6,000 mm/min, 1 pass, bidirectional, a maximum feed of 60,000 mm/min reads "About 1 min 40 s" (10,000 mm / 6,000 mm/min = 100 s, plus 9.9 mm / 60,000 mm/min = 0.01 s) and "Burn 10.00 m · travel 10 mm".

22. Given the Toolpath view's play bar (`0029` criterion 37), then the slider runs over the job's time including raster rows, and the head position moves along the row it is on; the drawn lengths up to the head equal the file's up to that move within 0.01 mm. Test: slider at half of a raster job reads half the total within 0.5 s.

### Engraving a gradient fill

The gradient itself (paint switch, colours, handles, drawing, SVG) is `0124`; this story only engraves it. It adds no gradient field, no gradient value and no control on a shape.

23. Given a shape, open path or text object with a Gradient fill (`0124`, Linear or Radial, any stops), then it is a raster item when its operation is an Image operation (criterion 3), and nothing else about the shape changes: its fill paint, stroke, corner radius, clones and transform stay as `0124` and the earlier specs make them. There is no switch "engrave as dots" (Frage 228 A): the Gradient fill is the request.
24. Given the value of a dot of a gradient-filled shape, then it is the lightness of the gradient's colour at the dot's centre (criterion 6): white burns nothing, black burns fully, mid grey is mid. Test: Start `#000000FF`, End `#FFFFFFFF` (two stops, Linear), Angle 0, Grey mode, Lowest power 0 %: the leftmost dot burns at full power, the dot at half width at about half, the rightmost dot not at all; at Angle 90 the shade runs over the height as `0124` draws it. The same value feeds every dither mode.
25. Given the drawing, then what the maker sees is what burns: a laser page shows a Gradient fill as grey (`0124` criterion 10), the lightness this story reads, and the Toolpath view shows the dots (criterion 20). The stroke of the shape is drawn as always and never engraved by the raster; the stroke colour never matters for output (`0101` criterion 24).
26. Given a shape with a Gradient fill that is turned, scaled, mirrored or skewed (on a laser page, by a clone or a placed page), then the gradient moves with it (`0124` criterion 15) and the dots follow criterion 4.
27. Given a gradient-filled shape in any dither mode, then the dither turns the shade into dots as it does an image (a ramp burns more dots toward the black end); in Grey it varies the power as in Frage 229. Brightness, Contrast, Gamma and Invert apply to it exactly as to an image.
28. Given a gradient-filled shape in a Cut, Score, Engrave, Engrave + Outline or Cut-out operation or in no operation, then it burns no dot (the fill paint is only drawn; Engrave hatches it by the rule of criterion 3); given one inside an Image test grid's cell (criterion 44), then the grid's Image operation makes it a raster item.
29. Given the model, then this story stores no gradient: the Gradient fill is `0124`'s data. Pixels are never stored (criterion 38). The SVG export and import of the gradient are `0124` criteria 13 and 14.
30. Given a shape whose Gradient fill `0124` removes (the paint switched to None, Colour or Pattern), then it is no longer a raster item: in an Image operation it is a shape that does not burn (criterion 48), in Engrave it keeps hatching as before, without notice.

### Checks (gate)

31. Given a raster item whose frame lies wholly or partly outside the work area (the box of the item, tolerance 0.01 mm), then it blocks as `0029` criterion 4 (b) with the symbol `OctagonX`: "1 item lies outside the 400 × 300 mm bed." / "<n> items lie outside the 400 × 300 mm bed." counting the image like any other item; the count is a button that selects the offending items. The item's frame is used even where its edge is transparent (Proposal: simple and predictable).
32. Given an Image operation, then (a) no power or no speed is "No settings" as before; an Image operation always has an Interval (absent reads 0.10, anything else is Damaged settings, ADR 0056 §5), so there is no separate "no interval" check; (b) given Grey mode with Lowest power above the set's Power (also after a later material pick; typing it is allowed, Proposal), then every burned dot runs at the set's Power (the ramp collapses, it never inverts) and it warns with `TriangleAlert`: "Image 1: Lowest power is above Power." and the line selects and unfolds the row (UX E7).
33. Given Grey mode on a machine whose laser mode is M3 (constant power, `0026`), then it blocks: "Image 1 needs laser mode M4 for Grey." (symbol `OctagonX`; the line selects, unfolds the row and focuses Dither) (Frage 229).
34. Given the dot count of a raster item above 50 million (item frame area divided by Interval squared, all passes ignored; for example 400 x 300 mm at Interval 0.01 mm is 1.2 billion), then it blocks: "Photo 1 would need 120,000,000 dots; the limit is 50,000,000." naming the item by its name, a shape too (the button selects it; the line needs no instruction, UX E3). The limit is confirmed by the architect (ADR 0068 §7). Given any dot count at or below the limit, then no block.
35. Given an image whose pixels are finer than the Interval (several source pixels per dot), then nothing warns; given an image coarser than the Interval by more than a factor of two (the image's pixel pitch on the page, from its placement, larger than twice the Interval), then it warns with `TriangleAlert`: "1 image is coarser than its Interval." / "<n> images are coarser than their Interval." and the line selects those images (Proposal: the bilinear rule of criterion 6 hides it otherwise). Given several raster items (images and gradient shapes) of one operation whose frames overlap with an area above 0 (images by their parallelograms, shapes by their outline boxes, Proposal), then it warns once: "<n> items overlap and burn twice." (selects them; UX E4).
36. Given an image whose working image was reduced by `0058`, then it warns: "1 image was reduced and loses detail." / "<n> images were reduced and lose detail." (selects them). Given an image whose asset cannot be read (missing or damaged bytes, `0058` damage rules), then it is skipped like an image in `0029` criterion 18 and warns once: "1 image cannot be read and is skipped." / "<n> images cannot be read and are skipped." (every count has its singular form, UX E5). The skip is the Image kind's: in a vector kind an image needs no pixels for its frame rectangle (criterion 1) and still runs there without a warn line. The new lines come after the lines of `0029` criterion 5, in the order of criteria 31, 33, 34, 35, 36, then the line of criterion 32 (b).

### Budgets, determinism, storage

37. Budgets (thread CPU time, `curvyo-editor-wasm/tests/cpu_time/` and `curvyo-laser-core`; the architect confirms the limits; Proposal): (a) the dots and rows of a 200 x 200 mm raster item at Interval 0.1 mm (2,000 rows of 2,000 dots, 4 million dots) build within 200 ms in Threshold and Ordered mode, within 350 ms in the four error diffusions; (b) the GRBL writer turns the resulting rows into the file within 250 ms for a worst-case dither (about 2 million runs); (c) a job with a 200 x 200 mm image at 0.1 mm and 50 Cut items builds within the sum of the above and `0029` criterion 36 (a); (d) the Operations view's preview of an edited Image setting (criterion 20) on the same item is ready within 50 ms, and the app's whole frame after that edit or its undo (the Operations tab, the output group and the canvas) takes within 50 ms, the job's rest built in following animation frames of at most 16 ms each (2026-11-19, criterion 12); (e) pan and zoom of the Toolpath view with the same job take within 16 ms per pointer move; (f) the checks recompute within 16 ms after an edit that does not change dots (a number, a material, Output, a switch other than an Image setting); (g) opening a project with 20 images of 5 megapixels each and no Image operation takes no longer than before this slice (the dots are built lazily, only when a raster operation runs or is previewed).
38. Given where the work is kept, then no processed pixel, dot, run or row is stored in the project: the job and the preview are derived from the image bytes in `assets/` (`0058`), the page and the operation's settings, cached in memory by the page's revision per operation (`0029` budget d, ADR 0064 §9) and dropped when the revision changes. The project file therefore does not grow by engraving, and the same image used by two operations with different settings is stored once. Test: save a project with an Image operation holding an image, engrave it, save again: the two `.curvyo` files are byte-identical when nothing changed.
39. Given the same project and settings, then two exports are byte-identical (`0029` criterion 31).
40. Given golden files, then dot maps and G-code are checked: dot maps in `curvyo-laser-core/tests/fixtures/raster/` (small fixtures, 16 x 16 grey ramp images as PNG and their expected dot maps as text, one per mode and per setting: `ramp_threshold_50`, `ramp_ordered`, `ramp_floyd_steinberg`, `ramp_jarvis`, `ramp_atkinson`, `ramp_stucki`, `ramp_grey`, `ramp_brightness_gamma_invert`, `alpha_edge`, `scan_angle_30`) and GRBL output in `curvyo-laser-core/tests/fixtures/output/grbl/` (`raster_threshold.gcode`, `raster_ordered.gcode`, `raster_floyd_steinberg.gcode`, `raster_jarvis.gcode`, `raster_atkinson.gcode`, `raster_stucki.gcode`, `raster_grey_m4.gcode`, `raster_unidirectional.gcode`, `raster_scan_angle.gcode`, `raster_overscan.gcode`, `raster_gradient_shape.gcode` (an ellipse with a Gradient fill), `raster_passes.gcode`, `raster_image_frame_kinds.gcode` (one image in Cut, Score, Cut-out, Engrave and Engrave + Outline: frame lines and hatch, no dots), `raster_image_and_cut.gcode` (dots in Image plus the frame in Cut), `raster_m3_constant_power.gcode`, `raster_origin_corner_tl.gcode`, and `raster_white_skipped.gcode`, which proves that white writes nothing). Each G-code golden is read back by the tester-written GRBL parser of `0029` criterion 31, which checks the extent, the sum of burn lengths and the order independently of the generator, and the rows' burned length equals the burned dot count times the Interval (within 0.01 mm). The golden of `0029` `image_skipped.gcode` is replaced by `raster_threshold.gcode` (it no longer holds).
41. Given that all this is derived, then there is no new format for the job. The format change is the new `op_options` keys (criterion 13) and the value `image` of the operation `kind` register (`0101`); project format 50 is unreleased and not yet merged, so there is no bump for the new kind and no migration; the Gradient fill's format is `0124`'s: the architect decides in `adrs.md` (ADR 0069 §10: yes, one bump, the next free number at merge after `0124`'s, plus material test record format 3, which holds the material's fourth set per laser, Image (Frage 270 B; format 3 is unreleased, so the set is added to it with no migration: a record without an Image set reads as no Image set), and (Frage 237, built A) also the Image test's kind and dither in the recipe; no migration: a file saved before this slice opens unchanged, an operation without the new keys reads the defaults, and Curvyo has no users yet, so old-file compatibility is not a concern). Golden project files: `raster_engraving_vNN.curvyo` (an Image operation with an image, a linked clone of it, a gradient-filled ellipse and every setting, and an Engrave that holds the image and hatches its frame) and `raster_engraving_damaged_vNN.curvyo` (a damaged `dither` value, `gamma` 4.01, `dither` on a Cut); NN is taken at merge; material record goldens `format3_image_test.json` and `format2_reads_solid.json`. The Gradient fill's own damage rules and goldens are `0124` criterion 22.
42. Given crates and dependencies, then the dot grid, the adjustments and the dither run in a `*-core` crate (all pixel work lives in `curvyo-laser-core`, `job_raster_*` modules; its one new edge is to `curvyo-image-core` for the decoded working image, plus `libm` for the gamma table, ADR 0068 §1; the job itself stays pixel-free in the project file), with no filesystem, network, clock, thread or UI, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, building for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`; this slice adds no crate and no new dependency to the lock file (the two edges above are the architect's, ADR 0068), no `unsafe`, and no trait without a second implementation.
43. Given the whole slice, then there is no device I/O, no network and no telemetry (`0029` criterion 35).

### The Image operation and linked clones

46. Given a raster item that is reached through a clone, then it burns like the original: (a) an image drawn directly on a laser page, a linked clone of it (`0049`; created by Clone), a clone of that clone, a clone of a group that holds the image, and an image that is a member of a placed page (`0106`) are all images for this story; the clone or placed page is assigned as a whole (`0111` criterion 14), and every image inside it runs in the Image operation by the nearest rule (`0101` criterion 12), at the place and the transform it has on the laser page (criterion 4); (b) the Operations view shows the clone's dots in the pale orange look and its Image glyph, the Toolpath view and the file hold its rows, and the checks and the count of the operations row include it; (c) a clone follows its original: editing the source image's asset or its settings changes the clone's dots after the revision, with no extra step. Acceptance case (the customer's bug, PR #394): draw a page, place an image, make a linked clone with Clone and move it beside the original, select both, press the Image kind button once: the Operations tab shows one row "Image 1" with two items, the Operations view shows dots on both, the Toolpath view and the GRBL file hold two raster blocks, and the clone's rows equal the original's translated by the clone's offset (same dots, byte-identical rows apart from the start point). Test the same with the clone as the only item, with a clone of a group of two images, and with an image inside a placed page. A clone that points at a deleted or unreadable source image is skipped and warned as in criterion 36 in an Image operation; in the other kinds it runs as its frame rectangle where the frame is still known.
47. Given the kind **Image**, then: (a) the Select bar's kind buttons are six, in the order Cut, Score, Engrave, Engrave + Outline, Cut-out, Image (`0111` criterion 1), the new one symbol-only with the dot-ramp glyph of Words (Proposal; UX E8), tooltip and accessible name "Image", pressing it with a selection of items assigns them to the page's last Image operation or creates "Image <N>" if none exists (the rules of `0111` criteria 3 to 7, Shift+press creates a new one); (b) an Image operation stands in the Marking section of the Operations tab beside Engrave, Engrave + Outline and Score, with Show and Output as every kind; (c) the operation kind is stored as `image` in the `kind` register (criterion 41); an older build reads the value as an Unknown operation (`0101`); (d) its folded row's third line shows the numbers (Power, Speed, Passes, Interval) and then " · <dither>" ("Floyd" for Floyd-Steinberg, the other names whole; Grey adds its range, "Grey 10–35 %", UX E12), and its unfolded row shows the numbers and the settings of criterion 11, no other kind's operation-only settings but Bidirectional fill, Scan angle, Overscanning and Z offset; (e) the Operations tab's selection of an item with an Image operation shows the Image glyph (the dot ramp); kinds differ by symbol, never by colour alone (`0101` criterion 18). Test: select an image, press Image: one operation "Image 1", Dither Floyd-Steinberg; press Engrave: one operation "Engrave 1" that hatches the image as its frame rectangle (criterion 1).
48. Given a leaf in an Image operation that is not a raster item (a shape, open path, text object, group member or clone whose fill paint is Colour, Pattern or None), then it burns nothing there (Proposal: LightBurn's Image mode does the same; the maker uses Engrave for filled vector areas) and the checks warn once with `TriangleAlert`: "1 item in Image 1 is not a picture and does not burn." / "<n> items in Image 1 are not pictures and do not burn." (counted by leaf, naming the operation; the line selects those items and unfolds the row; singular form as criterion 36). The line stands after the lines of criterion 36 and before the line of criterion 32 (b). Test: a black rectangle (Colour fill) assigned to Image: the Toolpath view and the file have no move for it, and the warn line appears; the same rectangle with a Gradient fill burns. The line is for vector leaves only: an image never causes it in any kind (criterion 1; UX E9 is void). On the canvas such a leaf has the Not assigned look with the Image glyph in muted ink (UX E10).

### Test grids

44. Given the test grid of `0112` (Frage 231 A, answered, changed by the customer's review of 2026-11-19; needs `0124`), then its kind pick gains a fourth kind **Image** beside Cut, Score and Engrave (Proposal; the Engrave grid loses the Fill and Dither fields it had in the earlier draft and is the vector hatch grid of `0112` again). ## Out of scope

- Offset fill and the vector Fill hatch (already exist: `0029` criterion 14; not changed).
- Image optimisation (automatic, AI-assisted, manual: a test photo is hard to recognise when engraved): the backlog story `0133` `image-optimisation`; this story gives the seven dither modes and the four adjustments only.
- Raster of a filled vector shape (a Colour fill burning as a solid dot area) in an Image operation: Engrave does that; the Image kind ignores such leaves and warns (criterion 48).
- **Future idea (customer, 2026-11-19, not now): Cut-out that follows the contour of an image with a transparent background** (today Cut-out cuts the frame rectangle, criterion 1); it belongs with image optimisation, backlog `0133`.
- Rotary (`0119`), camera (`0120`), print and cut (`0121`), machine control (`0118`): none of them is needed; raster engraving works on the plain job.
- Dot mode (single pulses), halftone and newsprint, other dithers than the six of Frage 230 B, a tone curve editor, sharpen, blur, noise reduction, background removal; per-image crop and per-image adjustments (the adjustments belong to the operation, not to the image object; the "Image block in the Style tab" of `0058` criterion 35 stays a backlog Proposal).
- A scan offset (backlash compensation of bidirectional rows, "line shift"), a variable Interval across the image, a dot gain compensation: later, with machine tests.
- A second output format's raster mode: the writer of any later format implements the raster rows of criterion 14 as part of adding that format (`0029` criterion 39); this story writes GRBL only.
- Engraving the fill colour of a shape by its lightness (Frage 228 B), the gradient design, a gradient on a stroke (the gradient design, radial, stops, text and open paths are all `0124`).
- Sending the job (`0064`), framing, the live Home button.
- Image resolution changes by the maker (resampling the object), tracing, trace-to-engrave; the image stays as imported.
- Anything about CNC, plotter or embroidery.

## Decided questions

Frage 227 to 231 were asked on 2026-11-17 and answered the same day; Frage 270 and 271 were answered on 2026-11-19.

- **Frage 227 (RASTER-GRADIENT): C (customer, 2026-11-17).** Gradient is a fill paint of **every closed shape**, not only a rectangle, and it is built first as its own story, `0124-gradient-fill`, because it would blow up this one. The options A (rectangle only, Start/End/Angle in burn strength) and B ("Generate gradient" makes an image) are dropped. This story reads the Gradient fill of `0124` as lightness (criteria 3, 23 to 30).
- **Frage 228 (RASTER-SHAPES): A (answered, the default).** A shape with a solid colour keeps the vector hatch at Interval (`0029`) in Engrave; only images and shapes with a Gradient fill become dots, and only in an Image operation (2026-11-19). A solid-colour shape in an Image operation is ignored with a warning (criterion 48, Proposal).
- **Frage 229 (RASTER-GREY): A (answered, the default).** A seventh Dither mode, Grey, burns each dot with a power between Lowest power (default 0 %) and the set's power according to its darkness; white burns nothing; needs laser mode M4, with M3 it blocks (criterion 33).
- **Frage 230 (RASTER-DITHERS): B (customer, 2026-11-17).** Threshold, Ordered (8 x 8 Bayer), Floyd-Steinberg, Jarvis, Atkinson and Stucki, plus Grey (229 A). New operations start with Floyd-Steinberg (Proposal).
- **Frage 231 (RASTER-GRID): A (answered, the default; changed 2026-11-19 by the customer's review, Proposal).** The Engrave grid's bar was to get Fill and Dither; with the Image kind it is now a fourth test grid kind Image with Dither, every cell a Gradient-filled rectangle engraved at the cell's Interval and Power; the Engrave grid is the vector hatch grid of `0112` again. Dither is never an axis. It depends on `0124` (criteria 44, 45).

- **Frage 270 (IMAGE-SET): B (customer, 2026-11-19).** The material gets a fourth numbers set per laser, **Image** (power, speed, passes, interval), like Cut, Score and Engrave. An Image operation reads it, a worked Image test cell writes it, the Materials tab shows four sets, and material test record format 3 (unreleased) holds it with no migration (criteria 2, 13, 41, 45). Options A (the Engrave set serves Image) and C (no set, typed only) are dropped.
- **Frage 271 (IMAGE-OTHER-KINDS): A (customer, 2026-11-19), with the clarification "strictly".** In Cut, Score, Cut-out, Engrave and Engrave + Outline an image is treated like a rectangle of its frame: Cut and Score trace it, Cut-out cuts it, Engrave hatches it, Engrave + Outline hatches and traces it; no pixel, no warn line (criterion 1). This replaces Frage 226 A of `0029` ("an image in Engrave runs nothing").
