# Raster engraving: engrave images and gradient rectangles as rows of dots, with dither modes, from an Engrave operation

Status: Draft
Priority: Should
Origin: Customer (earlier wish, kept in the backlog since `0063`: "image engraving with dithering, a gradient rectangle for tests, an interval test with dithering and crosshatch"; R-MFG-LASER-001 "raster engrave fills"). Everything marked **(Proposal)** is the product owner's and vetoable at the demo. Frage 227 to 231 are open (see "Open customer questions"); each has a default, so the story is Ready for the architect and the UX engineer as written, and a different answer changes only the criteria named under the question.

## User value

As a maker with a laser I want to engrave a photo or a shaded area on wood, slate or anodised metal with the same operation list I already use, choosing how the grey values become laser dots and seeing the result before I burn, so that I do not need a second program for pictures, and I want a gradient rectangle that I can engrave to find the power or the dither that suits my material.

**What other tools do.** LightBurn has an Image layer mode with a dialog of Threshold, Ordered, Atkinson, Dither, Stucki, Jarvis, Newsprint, Halftone and Grayscale, plus brightness, contrast and gamma in a separate "Adjust image" window; the result is previewed only in its own preview window; there is no gradient object, so power tests are made by importing a bitmap. xTool and Glowforge convert pictures behind a wizard, and the settings cannot be reused on a second picture. Inkscape's laser plug-ins ignore pixels. **We do better:** the picture stays an ordinary object and is assigned to Engrave like a shape (`0101`); its dither mode and adjustments are the Engrave operation's own settings and are in the same unfolded row as Interval and Scan angle (`0111`); the Operations view and the Toolpath view show the exact dots that burn; a gradient rectangle is a shape you draw with the Rectangle tool, and the job, the time and the file are format-neutral exactly as `0029` made them.

## Words (shown in the UI)

- **Raster item:** a leaf that is engraved as dots: an image, or a rectangle whose fill paint is Gradient (criterion 23). Every other leaf keeps the vector hatch of `0029` criterion 14.
- **Dot:** one burn position of a raster item: a square of Interval by Interval on the dot grid (criterion 5). A dot is burned (laser on) or not; in Grey mode a burned dot has its own power.
- **Dot grid:** the dots of one raster item, in rows at the Scan angle, Interval apart.
- **Run:** a stretch of neighbouring burned dots of one row that have the same power; the unit the move list and the Toolpath view use.
- **Dither mode:** how the grey values of a raster item become dots: **Threshold** (a dot burns if the item is darker than the threshold), **Ordered** (a fixed 8 x 8 Bayer pattern), **Floyd-Steinberg** and **Jarvis** (error diffusion), and **Grey** (the dot's power follows its grey value, Frage 229).
- **Image settings:** the group of the unfolded Engrave or Engrave + Outline row that holds Dither mode, Threshold, Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, Invert and Lowest power (criterion 11). They are operation-only settings in the sense of `0111`: they belong to the operation, never to a material.
- **Gradient fill:** the fill paint "Gradient" of a rectangle: a linear shade from a Start burn strength to an End burn strength along an Angle (criterion 23). Burn strength is 0 % (white, nothing burns) to 100 % (black, full power).
- **Gradient rectangle:** a rectangle with a Gradient fill.
- **Raster engrave:** what an Engrave or Engrave + Outline operation does with a raster item.

One-line meanings (customer rule: the meaning of a new operation before it is built):

- **Raster engrave (not a new kind; what Engrave does with a picture):** "Burns the picture as rows of dots: darker parts burn, lighter parts do not."
- **Dither mode:** "Chooses how the grey tones become burned and unburned dots."
- **Gradient fill:** "A shade from one burn strength to another, to engrave a power ramp or a soft area."

## Acceptance criteria

"Laser page", "item", "leaf", "Not assigned", "nearest rule", "Cuts last" are as in `0101`; "operation-only settings", "Interval", "Scan angle", "Bidirectional fill", "Overscanning", "Crosshatch" as in `0111`; "job", "burn move", "travel move", "Toolpath view", "checks", "block", "warn", "effective numbers", "output format" as in `0029`; "test grid", "set" as in `0112`; "working image" as in `0058`. All numbers are mm unless a unit is named. No operation kind is added: raster engrave is what Engrave and Engrave + Outline do with a raster item.

### What runs

1. Given an image in an Engrave operation (assigned by the item or by the nearest rule, `0101` criterion 12), then it is raster engraved: the Toolpath view and the file hold its dots (criterion 14). This replaces `0029` criterion 18 ("silently ignored"). Given an image in Cut or Score, then it still writes its frame as a line and no pixel (`0101` criterion 19, 20; unchanged).
2. Given an image in Engrave + Outline, then the dots run with the Engrave set (power, speed, passes, Interval) and the image's frame runs as a closed line with the Score set (`0028` criterion 25; `0101` criterion 22), the dots first. Given Cut-out, nothing of the pixels runs (`0101` criterion 23).
3. Given a leaf that is a rectangle with a Gradient fill (criterion 23) in an Engrave operation, then it is raster engraved like an image: its dots follow the gradient, clipped to the rectangle (rounded corners included) and its outline is not drawn; in Engrave + Outline the outline runs with the Score set; in Cut, Score and Cut-out only its outline runs and the fill paint never counts (`0101` criterion 24).
4. Given a raster item that is a clone, a member of a placed page or a clone's member, or carries a transform (rotation, scale, mirror, skew), then the dots are those of the item as it stands on the laser page (`0029` criterion 17), so a mirrored image engraves mirrored on the bed. The opacity of an image node never changes the dots (Proposal: opacity is a view setting, `0050`).

### The dot grid and the resampling rule

5. Given a raster item, then its dot grid is square: row spacing equals dot size equals the Interval of the operation (`0111` criterion 19; range 0.01 to 2.00). Rows are parallel to the Scan angle (0 degrees: parallel to the bed's X axis, positive angles counter-clockwise on the page, `0111` criterion 20). Dot centres of the first row lie half an Interval inside the item's frame at the Scan angle (the rule of `0029` criterion 14), and a dot is part of the grid when its centre lies inside the item's frame (the frame of an image; the outline of a gradient rectangle, curve-flattened as `0029` criterion 17). No dot is ever placed outside the frame.
6. Given the value of a dot, then it is the average grey of the source over the dot's square when the source is finer than the dot grid (several source pixels per dot: area average, box filter), and a bilinear interpolation between the four nearest source pixels when the source is coarser (fewer source pixels than dots, so a small image engraved at a fine Interval stays smooth and not blocky) (Proposal). Grey is the luminance of the pixel's sRGB value (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B on gamma-encoded values, as a fixed integer weighting, Proposal: no colour management, `0022`). A pixel's alpha blends the pixel over white: a fully transparent pixel is white and never burns; a half transparent black pixel reads as mid grey.
7. Given the source pixels of an image, then they are those of the working image of `0058` (RGBA8, at most 16 megapixels and 8,192 pixels a side); a picture that `0058` reduced (original size larger than working size, `is_reduced`) is engraved from the reduced working image, and the gate warns (criterion 36). Nothing of the source is changed or stored by the engraving.
8. Given the same image, settings and page, then the dots are identical on every platform (native and wasm): the grey value, brightness, contrast, gamma, ordered matrix and both error diffusions use integer or fixed-point arithmetic only, so no floating-point rounding can differ. Test: the golden dot maps of criterion 40 are byte-identical on both targets.

### Adjustments and dither modes

9. Given the order of the work on a dot's value, then it is: resample (criterion 6), Invert (if on, grey becomes 255 minus grey), Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, then the dither mode. Brightness is -100 to 100 % (whole numbers, default 0), Contrast -100 to 100 % (default 0), Gamma 0.10 to 4.00 (two decimals, default 1.00; above 1 lightens mid tones). Test: Brightness +100 % engraves nothing in any mode; Invert on a black square engraves nothing and on a white square burns the whole square.
10. Given the dither modes, then: **Threshold** burns a dot when its grey is below the Threshold (0 to 100 %, whole numbers, default 50 %; the field is part of the group and applies to Threshold only); **Ordered** compares the grey with the 8 x 8 Bayer matrix at the dot's row and column of the dot grid; **Floyd-Steinberg** and **Jarvis** spread the error of each dot to its not yet decided neighbours with the classic weights (7/16, 3/16, 5/16, 1/16 and the twelve weights over 48), row by row from the first row, always in the direction of increasing dot column (not in the direction the head moves, so Bidirectional fill never changes the dots; Proposal), dots outside the frame take no error. **Grey** is Frage 229. A dot of an area that is completely white in any mode is never burned, and in every mode a fully black area burns every dot (Threshold above 0 %, Ordered, Floyd-Steinberg, Jarvis). Test: a 16-step grey ramp gives a monotonic number of burned dots in the four dither modes (more darkness, at least as many burned dots) and black burns fully, white burns nothing.
11. Given an unfolded Engrave or Engrave + Outline row, then under the operation-only settings of `0111` criterion 20 it shows the Image settings group, in this order: Dither mode (a closed pick with the dither modes, Floyd-Steinberg by default for a new operation, Proposal), Threshold (shown with Threshold only), Brightness, Contrast, Gamma, Invert (an icon toggle, symbol `Contrast`, tooltip "Invert", off), and Lowest power (shown with Grey only). The group is shown only while the operation holds at least one raster item (so a row that engraves only shapes shows exactly what `0111` built); the settings stay stored while the group is hidden. Fields are plain number fields with the unit inside ("%", none for Gamma), no sliders, and no captions beyond the field name, as `0111`; the pick is the closed list of `0111`. Crosshatch, Bidirectional fill, Scan angle, Overscanning, Interval and Z offset keep their place and meaning.
12. Given a value in the Image settings outside its range, then it is refused with the range in the field's message and nothing is written; a value with more decimals than the field holds is rounded to its step; a valid change is one undo step "Edit settings" and recomputes the Operations view, the Toolpath view and the checks in the same frame as far as the budget of criterion 37 allows (criterion 20 for the preview). Undo restores the setting; no pixel is ever a history step.
13. Given the settings, then they are stored on the operation in the `op_options` register of `0111` as new known keys (no damage rule applies to them): `dither` (`threshold`, `ordered`, `floyd_steinberg`, `jarvis`, `grey`), `threshold`, `brightness`, `contrast`, `gamma`, `invert`, `lowest_power`; a missing key reads as its default; an unknown value of `dither`, or a value out of range, is the damage of `0111` criterion 25 (the operation does not run; one warn line "<n> operations have damaged settings and do not run.", `0029` criterion 33). A material pick, "Use material", "Reset to material" and a material edit never change them (`0111` criterion 23, `0028`). Material sets stay: power, speed, passes, interval.

### The move list, time and the file

14. Given a raster item, then the job builder emits its dots as **raster rows**: one entry per row of the dot grid, each holding the row's start point, its direction and the ordered runs of the row (start offset in dots, length in dots, power in %), not one move per dot and not one point per dot. The job stays format-neutral (`0029` criterion 39): a raster row names no G-code, no S value and no feed word; one function builds the Toolpath view and every writer's input (`0029` criterion 9). The ordering inside an operation is by raster item in stacking order, bottom first (Proposal: raster items are not nested shapes, so the inner-first rule of `0029` criterion 22 does not apply to them), and a raster item's rows after the operation's vector paths of an Engrave + Outline.
15. Given a row of runs, then the head sweeps the row between its first and last burned dot, with the laser off over unburned dots; a gap of unburned dots longer than 5 mm inside the row is a laser-off travel move at the machine's maximum feed (Proposal for the limit, the architect confirms); a row with no burned dot is skipped and writes nothing; leading and trailing unburned stretches of a row are not travelled. The step from one row to the next is a travel move of one Interval (sideways) at the maximum feed. Test: a half-white image writes no move over the white half.
16. Given Bidirectional fill on (default), then the rows alternate direction and no travel back is added (the step to the next row is at the end where the head stands); off, every row runs in the same direction and the head returns with a laser-off travel move (the rule of `0029` criterion 15). Given Overscanning, then each row is extended on both ends by the stated percentage of the run-out of `0029` criterion 15 with the laser off, and the first burned dot of every row is burned at full feed. Given Crosshatch on, then it is ignored for a raster item (it would double-burn the same dots; Proposal; it applies to the vector hatch of the other items of the operation). Given the Scan angle, then the rows are turned as in criterion 5.
17. Given passes (1 to 20), then the whole operation is written once per pass, raster rows included (the rule of `0029` criterion 21), and the dither is not recomputed between passes: the same dots burn each pass.
18. Given the feed, then a raster row is written at the operation's speed with the effective numbers of `0029` criterion 2; the sweep is written at constant feed over the row's span (no acceleration modelled); with a speed above the machine's maximum feed the written feed is the maximum and the gate warns (`0029` criterion 21).
19. Given the GRBL output format and laser mode M4 (dynamic), then a row is written as one `G1` per run with `S` set to the run's power (S = round(power % x S-max / 100), `0029` criterion 25) and `S0` over unburned stretches inside the row, `F` only where it changes; a run of the same power as its neighbour is one `G1`; laser-off travel moves are `G0` as in `0029`. Given laser mode M3 (constant), then every burned run is `M3 S<n>` ... `M5` per run, and unburned gaps are `G0` (as `0029` criterion 25 requires for travel); Grey is then a block (criterion 33). The line count of the file is the number of runs plus the number of rows plus the rows' travel moves; a file is never written with one line per dot. The file stays free of date, path and random numbers (`0029` criterion 24).
20. Given the Toolpath view, then a raster item is drawn from the same rows: burn runs in the Engrave look (`0101` criterion 18, pale orange, one device pixel wide at least) and the row travel as thin dashed lines; when the view is zoomed so far out that rows are closer than one device pixel, the item is drawn as its shaded dot area (the dots rendered at the screen's resolution, same data), and the start of the operation keeps its numbered ring and the item gets one arrowhead at its first row (not one per row; Proposal). While the maker edits an Image setting, the Operations view draws the item as its dots in the Engrave look (pale orange dots on a transparent ground) at a resolution of at most one dot per device pixel, recomputed within the budget of criterion 37 (d), and the Toolpath view and the file always use the full dot grid. Nothing is told by colour alone: a raster item in the Operations view carries the Engrave glyph at its corner as `0101` criterion 18 sets.
21. Given the time estimate (`0029` criterion 10), then a raster item's time is the sum of its rows' lengths (first burned dot to last, plus overscan) divided by the feed, times passes, plus the travel of criterion 15 divided by the maximum feed; a raster row of Interval 0.1 mm and 0 dots burned adds nothing. The total and the run time of the operation's row (`0029` criterion 2) include it; "Burn <length> · travel <length>" counts the row lengths as burn and the row steps and long gaps as travel. Test: a 100 mm x 10 mm solid black square at Interval 0.1 mm (100 rows of 100 mm, 99 sideways steps of 0.1 mm = 9.9 mm), speed 6,000 mm/min, 1 pass, bidirectional, a maximum feed of 60,000 mm/min reads "About 1 min 40 s" (10,000 mm / 6,000 mm/min = 100 s, plus 9.9 mm / 60,000 mm/min = 0.01 s) and "Burn 10.00 m · travel 10 mm".

22. Given the Toolpath view's play bar (`0029` criterion 37), then the slider runs over the job's time including raster rows, and the head position moves along the row it is on; the drawn lengths up to the head equal the file's up to that move within 0.01 mm. Test: slider at half of a raster job reads half the total within 0.5 s.

### The gradient rectangle

23. Given the Rectangle tool's bar or the Style tab (where fill paint is chosen, `0071` criterion on `fill_paint`), then a rectangle (a primitive rectangle, with corner radius, not a path) has a fill paint pick with the values Colour (the solid colour), Pattern (`0071`) and Gradient (Frage 227). Choosing Gradient shows the fields Start (%), End (%) and Angle (degrees), plain number fields with the unit inside, in the Style tab where the pattern fields are today; no sliders, no stops list, no colour. Defaults: Start 0 %, End 100 %, Angle 0 degrees. Ranges: Start and End 0 to 100 %, whole numbers; Angle -180.0 to 180.0, one decimal, 0 meaning the shade runs from the rectangle's left edge (Start) to its right edge (End), positive angles counter-clockwise (Proposal).
24. Given a Gradient fill, then it is a linear shade of the burn strength along the Angle over the rectangle's own box (the span is the box projected on the direction, so Start is at the first corner reached and End at the last); the value is linear in burn strength between Start and End, with no stops between (customer lesson: no invented controls; Proposal). Test: Start 0 %, End 100 %, Angle 0, Grey mode: the leftmost dot burns at 0 % and the rightmost at 100 % of the set power; at Angle 90 the shade runs from the bottom edge to the top.
25. Given a Gradient fill, then the drawing shows it as a ramp from white (Start 0 %) to black (End 100 %) in the document's greys (no other colour is introduced; the render uses the existing document grey, `docs/design-system.md` "no invented colours"); Start above End is allowed (the ramp runs the other way). Given a rectangle that has a Gradient fill and a stroke, then the stroke is drawn as always and never engraved by the raster; the stroke colour never matters for output (`0101` criterion 24).
26. Given a rectangle with a Gradient fill that is turned, scaled or mirrored (on a laser page or by a clone or placed page), then the gradient turns, scales and mirrors with it (it belongs to the rectangle's own box) and the dots follow criterion 4.
27. Given the gradient rectangle in Threshold, Ordered, Floyd-Steinberg or Jarvis mode, then the dither turns the shade into dots as it does an image (a 0 % to 100 % ramp burns more dots to the right); in Grey it varies the power as in Frage 229. Brightness, Contrast, Gamma and Invert apply to it exactly as to an image.
28. Given a gradient rectangle in a Cut, Score or Cut-out operation or in no operation, then the fill paint is only drawn; given one inside a test grid's cell (Frage 231), then the grid's operations make it a raster item as criterion 3.
29. Given the model, then a Gradient fill is the style data of the rectangle, stored with the shape (`fill_paint` value `gradient` and the three numbers); pixels are never stored (criterion 38); the SVG export of `0024` writes the rectangle with a `linearGradient` of two grey stops (the one place the gradient shows outside Curvyo; Proposal: the architect confirms the SVG model) and the import reads it back as a Gradient fill only when it has exactly two grey stops and no other stop, otherwise it stays the plain-fill loss of `0024`.
30. Given the Gradient fill, then it can be set by the Rectangle tool's bar on a selected rectangle as well as by the Style tab; one change is one undo step "Edit gradient"; a rectangle turned into a path (`0003` "object to path") keeps the outline and loses the Gradient (the path has the solid colour of its fill paint) and the notice names it: "Gradient dropped.".

### Checks (gate)

31. Given a raster item whose frame lies wholly or partly outside the work area (the box of the item, tolerance 0.01 mm), then it blocks as `0029` criterion 4 (b) with the symbol `OctagonX`: "<n> items lie outside the 400 x 300 mm bed." counting the image like any other item; the count is a button that selects the offending items. The item's frame is used even where its edge is transparent (Proposal: simple and predictable).
32. Given an Engrave operation that holds a raster item and has no Interval (a damaged `op_settings` or a material set without one), then it blocks as `0029` criterion 4 (a): "Engrave 2 has no interval." and the line selects and unfolds the row. An operation that holds a raster item and has no power or no speed is "No settings" as before.
33. Given Grey mode on a machine whose laser mode is M3 (constant power, `0026`), then it blocks: "Engrave 1 needs laser mode M4 for Grey." (symbol `OctagonX`; the line selects and unfolds the row) (Frage 229).
34. Given the dot count of a raster item above 50 million (item frame area divided by Interval squared, all passes ignored; for example 400 x 300 mm at Interval 0.01 mm is 1.2 billion), then it blocks: "Image 1 would need 120,000,000 dots; the limit is 50,000,000. Raise the Interval." (Proposal for the limit; it bounds memory in wasm and the architect confirms in `adrs.md`). Given any dot count at or below the limit, then no block.
35. Given an image whose pixels are finer than the Interval (several source pixels per dot), then nothing warns; given an image coarser than the Interval by more than a factor of two (the image's pixel pitch on the page, from its placement, larger than twice the Interval), then it warns with `TriangleAlert`: "<n> images are coarser than their Interval." and the line selects those images (Proposal: the bilinear rule of criterion 6 hides it otherwise). Given several raster items of one operation whose frames overlap, then it warns once: "<n> images overlap and burn twice." (selects them).
36. Given an image whose working image was reduced by `0058`, then it warns: "<n> images were reduced and lose detail." (selects them). Given an image whose asset cannot be read (missing or damaged bytes, `0058` damage rules), then it is skipped like an image in `0029` criterion 18 and warns once: "<n> images cannot be read and are skipped." The order of the new lines in the checks is after the lines of `0029` criterion 5, in the order of criteria 35 and 36.

### Budgets, determinism, storage

37. Budgets (thread CPU time, `curvyo-editor-wasm/tests/cpu_time/` and `curvyo-laser-core`; the architect confirms the limits; Proposal): (a) the dots and rows of a 200 x 200 mm raster item at Interval 0.1 mm (2,000 rows of 2,000 dots, 4 million dots) build within 200 ms in Threshold and Ordered mode, within 350 ms in Floyd-Steinberg and Jarvis; (b) the GRBL writer turns the resulting rows into the file within 250 ms for a worst-case dither (about 2 million runs); (c) a job with a 200 x 200 mm image at 0.1 mm and 50 Cut items builds within the sum of the above and `0029` criterion 36 (a); (d) the Operations view's preview of an edited Image setting (criterion 20) on the same item is ready within 50 ms; (e) pan and zoom of the Toolpath view with the same job take within 16 ms per pointer move; (f) the checks recompute within 16 ms after an edit that does not change dots (a number, a material, Output, a switch other than an Image setting); (g) opening a project with 20 images of 5 megapixels each and no Engrave takes no longer than before this slice (the dots are built lazily, only when a raster operation runs or is previewed).
38. Given where the work is kept, then no processed pixel, dot, run or row is stored in the project: the job and the preview are derived from the image bytes in `assets/` (`0058`), the page and the operation's settings, cached in memory by the page's revision per operation (`0029` budget d, ADR 0064 §9) and dropped when the revision changes. The project file therefore does not grow by engraving, and the same image used by two operations with different settings is stored once. Test: save a project with an Engrave image, engrave it, save again: the two `.curvyo` files are byte-identical when nothing changed.
39. Given the same project and settings, then two exports are byte-identical (`0029` criterion 31).
40. Given golden files, then dot maps and G-code are checked: dot maps in `curvyo-laser-core/tests/fixtures/raster/` (small fixtures, 16 x 16 grey ramp images as PNG and their expected dot maps as text, one per mode and per setting: `ramp_threshold_50`, `ramp_ordered`, `ramp_floyd_steinberg`, `ramp_jarvis`, `ramp_grey`, `ramp_brightness_gamma_invert`, `alpha_edge`, `scan_angle_30`) and GRBL output in `curvyo-laser-core/tests/fixtures/output/grbl/` (`raster_threshold.gcode`, `raster_ordered.gcode`, `raster_floyd_steinberg.gcode`, `raster_jarvis.gcode`, `raster_grey_m4.gcode`, `raster_unidirectional.gcode`, `raster_scan_angle.gcode`, `raster_overscan.gcode`, `raster_gradient_rect.gcode`, `raster_passes.gcode`, `raster_engrave_outline.gcode`, `raster_m3_constant_power.gcode`, `raster_origin_corner_tl.gcode`, and `raster_white_skipped.gcode`, which proves that white writes nothing). Each G-code golden is read back by the tester-written GRBL parser of `0029` criterion 31, which checks the extent, the sum of burn lengths and the order independently of the generator, and the rows' burned length equals the burned dot count times the Interval (within 0.01 mm). The golden of `0029` `image_skipped.gcode` is replaced by `raster_threshold.gcode` (it no longer holds).
41. Given that all this is derived, then there is no new format for the job. The format changes are the new `op_options` keys (criterion 13) and the Gradient fill (criterion 29): the architect decides in `adrs.md` whether they need a `format_version` bump (Proposal: yes, one bump together, number taken at merge; no migration: a file saved before this slice opens unchanged, an operation without the new keys reads the defaults, and Curvyo has no users yet, so old-file compatibility is not a concern). Golden project files: `raster_engraving_v49.curvyo` (an Engrave with an image, a gradient rectangle and every setting) and `raster_engraving_damaged_v49.curvyo` (a damaged `dither` value, `gamma` 4.01, a Gradient fill with Start 101).
42. Given crates and dependencies, then the dot grid, the adjustments and the dither run in a `*-core` crate (`curvyo-laser-core` for the job; whether the pure pixel functions live in `curvyo-image-core` or in `curvyo-laser-core` is the architect's call, ADR 0066), with no filesystem, network, clock, thread or UI, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, building for `wasm32-unknown-unknown`; this slice adds no crate and no dependency (Proposal; the architect confirms), no `unsafe`, and no trait without a second implementation.
43. Given the whole slice, then there is no device I/O, no network and no telemetry (`0029` criterion 35).

### Test grids

44. Given the Engrave test grid of `0112` (Frage 231), then (default A) its bar gains two parameters that sit on no axis, shown only for the Engrave kind: **Fill** (a closed pick: Solid, Gradient; default Solid) and **Dither** (the dither modes of criterion 10 without Grey and shown only with Gradient; default Floyd-Steinberg). With Gradient, every cell of the grid is a gradient rectangle (Start 0 %, End 100 %, Angle 0) and the grid's derived operations carry the chosen Dither mode, the chosen Crosshatch and the cell's Interval; cell axes (Power, Speed, Passes, Interval) work as before, and Dither is never an axis (it is a list, not a number). The label operation is unchanged. Test: an Engrave grid with Fill Gradient, Interval on the X axis, Power on the Y axis and Dither Ordered makes cells that engrave a ramp at each cell's Interval and Power.
45. Given a test grid with Fill Solid, then it behaves exactly as `0112` built it (vector hatch with Interval; Crosshatch). The note of the record gains nothing; the cell that worked still sets power, speed, passes and interval of the material's set (`0112` criterion 24); Dither mode and Crosshatch stay operation settings and are not stored in a material (`0112` criterion 19).

## Out of scope

- Offset fill and the vector Fill hatch (already exist: `0029` criterion 14; not changed).
- Rotary (`0119`), camera (`0120`), print and cut (`0121`), machine control (`0118`): none of them is needed; raster engraving works on the plain job.
- Dot mode (single pulses), halftone and newsprint, Atkinson and Stucki dither (Frage 230 lists them as not chosen), a tone curve editor, sharpen, blur, noise reduction, background removal; per-image crop and per-image adjustments (the adjustments belong to the operation, not to the image object; the "Image block in the Style tab" of `0058` criterion 35 stays a backlog Proposal).
- A scan offset (backlash compensation of bidirectional rows, "line shift"), a variable Interval across the image, a dot gain compensation: later, with machine tests.
- A second output format's raster mode: the writer of any later format implements the raster rows of criterion 14 as part of adding that format (`0029` criterion 39); this story writes GRBL only.
- Engraving the fill colour of a shape by its lightness (Frage 228 B), a gradient on any shape other than a rectangle, radial gradients, gradient stops, a gradient on a stroke.
- Sending the job (`0064`), framing, the live Home button.
- Image resolution changes by the maker (resampling the object), tracing, trace-to-engrave; the image stays as imported.
- Anything about CNC, plotter or embroidery.

## Open customer questions

Frage 227 to 231, in the format of `CLAUDE.md` §1; also in the report to the lead.

**Frage 227 (RASTER-GRADIENT): Where does the gradient rectangle's shade live?**
- A: The rectangle gets a third fill paint, Gradient, next to Colour and Pattern (`0071`): Start %, End %, Angle, linear only, on the Rectangle primitive only. It stays a rectangle (corner radius, resize, clones, test-grid cells). It brings back a small part of the gradient fill that `0017` removed, as a burn strength and not a colour. Criteria 23 to 30.
- B: "Generate gradient" is a button that makes an image object (a 256-level PNG ramp) at the rectangle's size; the engraving is then plain image engraving and no fill changes. A new asset per size and angle, and editing the shade later means making a new image.
- C: Gradient is a fill paint of any closed shape (a circle with a shade), not only a rectangle.
- Recommendation: A. It is what you asked for (a rectangle with a gradient fill), stays editable, and keeps pixels out of the file.
- Default if unanswered: A.

**Frage 228 (RASTER-SHAPES): Does a plain filled shape engrave as dots?**
- A: No. A shape with a solid colour keeps the vector hatch at Interval (`0029`); only images and gradient rectangles become dots.
- B: Yes. The lightness of the fill colour decides the dots (a light grey fill burns fewer dots), shapes without a fill never.
- C: A switch on the Engrave operation, "Fill as: Lines / Dots", decides for all its shapes.
- Recommendation: A. The fill colour is a drawing decision, not a burn decision (`0101` criterion 24: fill, stroke and colour never count); a gradient is the way to ask for shades.
- Default if unanswered: A.

**Frage 229 (RASTER-GREY): Should the laser power follow the grey (Grey mode)?**
- A: Yes. A sixth Dither mode, Grey, burns each dot with a power between Lowest power (a field, default 0 %) and the set's power according to its darkness; white burns nothing. Needs laser mode M4; with M3 it blocks (criterion 33). The gradient rectangle then makes a real power ramp for tests.
- B: No. Only on/off dithers; a power ramp test is a grid of Power cells (`0112`).
- Recommendation: A. It costs one S value per run in the writer, and a continuous ramp is the clearest power test.
- Default if unanswered: A.

**Frage 230 (RASTER-DITHERS): Which dither modes?**
- A: Threshold, Ordered (8 x 8 Bayer), Floyd-Steinberg, Jarvis (and Grey, Frage 229). New operations start with Floyd-Steinberg.
- B: A plus Atkinson and Stucki (more error-diffusion looks, as LightBurn).
- C: Only Threshold and Floyd-Steinberg.
- Recommendation: A. Four distinct looks (hard, patterned, soft, smooth); more are one function each and can follow.
- Default if unanswered: A.

**Frage 231 (RASTER-GRID): Does the Engrave test grid get a gradient and a dither?**
- A: Yes. The Engrave grid's bar gets Fill (Solid, Gradient) and Dither (shown with Gradient); every cell is then a gradient rectangle engraved at the cell's Interval and Power with the chosen Dither and Crosshatch. Dither is never an axis. This answers your earlier wish for an interval test with dithering and crosshatch. Criteria 44, 45.
- B: Dither mode is an axis (a list of modes across the grid), Interval or Power on the other axis. A bigger change to the grid's axis model.
- C: No grid change in this story; the maker places gradient rectangles by hand and sets the operations.
- Recommendation: A. It reuses the existing derived operations and adds two bar fields.
- Default if unanswered: A.
