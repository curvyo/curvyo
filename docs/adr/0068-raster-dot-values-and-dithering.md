# ADR 0068: Raster dot values and dithering in `curvyo-laser-core`: the dot grid on the hatch lines, a grey pyramid per image, one tone table, seven integer modes, a `laser-core → image-core` edge

**Status:** Proposed 2026-11-18 (architect). No customer decision needed (inside the accepted modes of Frage 229 A and 230 B). Decisions marked **(Proposal)** are vetoable at the demo. Builds on [ADR 0064](0064-laser-job-builder-and-output-writers.md) §2 (units, determinism), §6 (hatch) and [ADR 0066](0066-gradient-fill-in-the-document-model.md) §4 (gradient colour and grey). The rows, output, preview, gate and stored keys are [ADR 0069](0069-raster-rows-output-and-settings.md). Adds one crate edge to [ADR 0011](0011-workspace-and-crate-layout.md) (note on acceptance). Spec: [`0123`](../../specs/0123-raster-engraving/specification.md) criteria 4 to 10, 24, 27, 34, 37, 42.

**Amended 2026-11-19 (architect, customer feedback on PR #394).** Raster items are the images (own, linked clones, members of cloned groups and of placed pages) and the gradient-filled shapes of an operation of the new kind **Image** (`op_kind` `image`, [ADR 0069](0069-raster-rows-output-and-settings.md) §13), run with the material's Image set (§14, Frage 270 B); Engrave and Engrave + Outline hatch only, and every kind but Image treats an image as its frame rectangle (Frage 271 A). Every rule below is unchanged; only who reaches it changes.

**In plain words (for the customer).** For every dot the laser may burn, Curvyo reads how dark the picture or the gradient is right there, applies Invert, Brightness, Contrast and Gamma, and then decides with the chosen mode whether the dot burns. Every step uses whole numbers, so the same project gives the same dots on every computer.

## Context

`curvyo-laser-core` has no pixels today: an image leaf reaches the job as its frame only (`job_input.rs`), and `0029` drops it from Engrave. The decoded working image (RGBA8, at most 16 Mpx, ADR 0017 §4) lives in the session's image cache as `Arc<WorkingImage>`, a type of `curvyo-image-core`, which depends on no Curvyo crate. Dots must be identical on native and wasm (criterion 8) and 4 million dots must cost at most 200 ms (350 ms with error diffusion, 37 a).

## Options

**Where the pixel work lives**

| Option | Why it won or lost |
|---|---|
| **A. All of it in `curvyo-laser-core` (`job_raster_*` modules), reading `WorkingImage` through a new `laser-core → image-core` edge (chosen)** | Dithers work on the laser's dot grid, gradients have no image, and the scan angle and Interval are laser settings: one domain, one crate. The edge brings no new crate into the lock file and only the type (and its pure `image` dependency, already wasm32) into laser-core. |
| B. Grey and dithering in `curvyo-image-core` | image-core would need the dot grid and the gradient colour (document-core), but it must depend on no Curvyo crate (ADR 0017 §4); and dithering is not decoding. Lost. |
| C. A new crate `curvyo-raster-core` | One consumer; `CLAUDE.md` §5. Lost. |

**Pixel access:** `Arc<WorkingImage>` in the job input (chosen; no copy) against an own pixel type in laser-core (a copy of up to 64 MB per image per build) or a borrowed slice (a lifetime through every job input type).

## Decision

1. **Modules** (`curvyo-laser-core`, each under 500 lines, one-sentence doc): `job_raster_grid.rs` (the dot grid of one raster item), `job_raster_sample.rs` (the grey value at a dot: image pyramid and gradient), `job_raster_tone.rs` (Invert, Brightness, Contrast and Gamma as one table), `job_raster_dither.rs` (the seven modes), `job_raster_rows.rs` (ADR 0069 §1). `job_input.rs`'s `LeafGeometry` gains `Image { frame, pixels: Option<Arc<WorkingImage>>, reduced: bool }` (`None`: unreadable, skipped and warned) and `Gradient { outlines, gradient: PlacedGradient }` for a leaf whose fill paints a gradient; the session builds both, and reads pixels only for the leaves of an Image operation (amended 2026-11-19). `Cargo.toml` of laser-core gains `curvyo-image-core` and `libm` (the workspace pin of ADR 0029 §5, already in the lock file; for decision 4 only).
2. **The dot grid** (criterion 5). The scan frame is the hatch frame of ADR 0064 §6 (`direction_of_degrees`, page Y down). In it the item's frame box gives rows at `v = low + I/2 + k·I` and columns at `u = low + I/2 + j·I`, `I` the Interval in µm; `(k, j)` are the grid's row and column for the Bayer matrix and the diffusions. A dot belongs to the grid when its centre lies inside the frame: the row's inside stretches are exactly `hatch`'s spans on that line over the item's rings (the image frame's four corners; a gradient shape's outlines flattened at 0.0095 mm, holes excluded by the nonzero rule, open outlines closed by a line, as ADR 0064 §4), so raster and hatch share one inside test. Coordinates as in the hatch: `f64 + - * /` from integer µm, rounded to µm at once.
3. **Dot values** (criteria 6, 7, 24). Grey `0` (black) to `255` (white), always in integers after the geometry.
   - **Image.** A grey plane per asset: each working-image pixel composited over white (straight alpha, `c·a + 255·(255 − a)` rounded) and weighted by `over_white_grey`'s integer weights (ADR 0066 §4). A pyramid of 2 x 2 integer box means (rounded half up, the `mip_level` rule of ADR 0017 §4). For an item, level `n` is the largest with `2^n` times the larger page size of one source pixel (along either image axis, from the frame) at most `I`; the dot's value is the bilinear blend of the four nearest level-`n` pixels around the dot centre mapped into the image by the inverse frame map (`f64 + - * /`), weights quantised to 1/256, edges clamped. Finer sources are thus box-averaged to within a factor of two of the dot, coarser ones are bilinear (level 0), as criterion 6 asks; **the area average is the power-of-two box nearest below the dot, not the exact dot footprint** (Proposal, criterion 6 says so: same smoothness, a fixed cost per dot). The grey pyramid is cached per asset hash in the session beside the working image and built on first raster use (37 g). The image node's opacity is not read (criterion 4).
   - **Gradient.** `over_white_grey(colour_at(dot centre))` of ADR 0066 §4, no resampling.
4. **Tone** (criterion 9). One `[u8; 256]` table per operation, built from the settings in this order, each step clamped to 0..255: Invert `255 − g`; Brightness `g + round(255·b / 100)`; Contrast `128 + (g − 128)·(100 + c) / (100 − c)` in integers with rounding half away from zero, `c = 100` giving `0` below 128 and `255` from 128; Gamma `round(255·(g / 255)^(1/γ))` with `libm::pow` (pure Rust, the same bits on every target), done 256 times per build. Per dot the work is one lookup. Brightness +100 % is then white everywhere and burns nothing in every mode (criterion 9's test).
5. **Modes** (criterion 10). Each dot gets a **level**: 0 is off, 1 to 255 burns; the binary modes give 0 or 255.
   - **Threshold:** burns iff `100·g < T·255`.
   - **Ordered:** the 8 x 8 Bayer matrix `M` (values 0 to 63, written out in the module), burns iff `128·g < (2·M[k mod 8][j mod 8] + 1)·255`; black always burns, white never.
   - **Floyd-Steinberg, Jarvis, Atkinson, Stucki:** rows in grid order, columns increasing (never the head's direction, so Bidirectional fill cannot change the dots). Error accumulators are `i32` in units of `1/D` grey (`D` = 16, 48, 8, 42); a dot's value is `v = D·g + acc`; it burns iff `v < 128·D`; its error `e = v − (burn ? 0 : 255·D)` goes to each not yet decided neighbour as `(e·w).div_euclid(D)` with the classic weights (Floyd-Steinberg 7, 3, 5, 1; Jarvis 7 5 / 3 5 7 5 3 / 1 3 5 3 1; Stucki 8 4 / 2 4 8 4 2 / 1 2 4 2 1; Atkinson 1 to each of six, so a quarter is dropped). A neighbour outside the row's inside stretches or the grid gets nothing. Three rolling rows of the grid width, so memory is `O(width)`. White in, zero error, nothing burns; black in, zero error, everything burns.
   - **Grey:** level `255 − g`. The power is ADR 0069 §1.
6. **Determinism** (criterion 8). Per dot only integers; `f64` only for geometry and the gradient's `t`, with `+ - * / sqrt` (ADR 0064 §2); `libm::pow` only for the 256 table entries. Golden dot maps (`curvyo-laser-core/tests/fixtures/raster/`, criterion 40, text: one line per row of `.` and `#`, or two hex digits per dot for Grey) freeze the arithmetic.
7. **Limit** (criterion 34) **confirmed: 50,000,000 dots per raster item**, estimated before any work as the item's box area in the scan frame over `I²`. Memory per item stays at most 6.25 MB in the binary modes and 50 MB in Grey (ADR 0069 §1). No job-wide limit (Proposal; the trigger is a measured wasm out-of-memory).
8. **Budgets** (criterion 37 a, d, g; thread CPU, `curvyo-editor-wasm/tests/cpu_time/`, native) **confirmed**: a 200 x 200 mm item at 0.1 mm (4 million dots) builds its levels in 200 ms in Threshold and Ordered and 350 ms in the diffusions, the grey pyramid of a 16 Mpx image included once; nothing raster runs while no Image operation is shown, previewed or exported, so opening 20 images costs what it costs today.

## Consequences

- `curvyo-laser-core` compiles the `image` crate through image-core; laser-core tests can build working images from PNG fixtures with `curvyo_image_core::decode_working`.
- A dot value never depends on the scan direction, the pass or the output format; changing a weight, the Bayer matrix or the tone formula is a change of every golden dot map and is reviewed as such.
- Large items take time in proportion: at the 50 million limit a build is about 12 times budget (a). If that is felt as a frozen page, moving the job build into the ADR 0023 worker is the follow-up; not built now.
- Rejected and why: image-core (no dot grid, no Curvyo dependency allowed), a new crate (one consumer), a pixel copy (memory), exact footprint averaging (variable cost per dot for no visible gain).
